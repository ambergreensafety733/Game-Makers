# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 2.2 — Balance Simulation 2: Marketing Active

This simulation tests whether marketing creates profitable strategic choices rather than a simple pay-to-win mechanism.

Four publishers were modelled across five business years with contrasting strategies. Figures are rounded design-model outcomes in $K, not claims of a completed human playtest.

## Companies

### Market Rocket Games — Amazon Advertiser
Strategy: China volume, Amazon Sponsored Ads, Christmas PPC, Budget/Standard pricing, early USA expansion.

### Direct Hit Games — Brand & Website Builder
Strategy: Website investment, mailing list/community, Standard pricing, direct margin, controlled inventory.

### Dragon Forge Games — Premium / PR
Strategy: Quality 4–5 Standard/Deluxe products, reviewer campaigns, awards, Premium pricing, European responsive production.

### Storyforge Publishing — KDP & Licensing
Strategy: books, translations, KDP, licences, low inventory and relatively low advertising.

# 1. Five-Year Design-Model Results

| Company | Y1 after-tax | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 | Y5 Brand |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Market Rocket | -$55K | $180K | $620K | $1,310K | $2,080K | 3 |
| Direct Hit | -$85K | $125K | $540K | $1,250K | $2,170K | 5 |
| Dragon Forge | -$120K | $90K | $470K | $1,180K | $2,030K | 4 |
| Storyforge | $35K | $150K | $360K | $720K | $1,180K | 3 |

Result: three physical-game strategies can plausibly cross the $2M target in Year 5. The low-risk KDP/licensing strategy remains profitable but does not reach the same ceiling without adding physical products or a major franchise.

# 2. Marketing Spend

Approximate annual marketing spend as a percentage of Business Revenue:

Market Rocket: 14% / 13% / 11% / 10% / 9%.
Direct Hit: 11% / 10% / 8% / 7% / 6%.
Dragon Forge: 12% / 11% / 9% / 8% / 7%.
Storyforge: 4% / 5% / 5% / 5% / 4%.

Finding: advertising naturally becomes a smaller percentage as companies scale, which feels commercially credible and avoids forcing repetitive late-game spending.

# 3. Heavy Advertising Test

Market Rocket attempted repeated Amazon campaigns.

When stock and market demand were available, a $20K +2 Appeal campaign was often profitable.

When the product already ranked first by Appeal, further advertising frequently generated no additional sales because Sales Claim, stock or market demand became the limiting factor.

The +5 temporary paid-advertising cap successfully prevents unlimited spending.

Important player lesson: **do not advertise merely because you can.**

# 4. Website / Brand Test

Direct Hit had the weakest Year 1 result because Cash was invested in Website and customer-building capability.

By Years 4–5, higher direct contribution plus Brand Appeal made the strategy extremely competitive.

Problem found: Brand 3 giving +1 Appeal to every product is powerful when a company has five/six products.

### Patch 2.2A
Brand 3: +1 Appeal to Launch/Growth products only.
Brand 4: +1 to all products; +1 Deal score.
Brand 5: +2 to Launch products; +1 to others; +1 Deal score.

This delays the portfolio-wide benefit.

# 5. Premium / PR Test

Dragon Forge's high Quality strategy consumed large early Cash reserves. Premium pricing was initially difficult, but Quality, Reputation, reviewers and awards later supported high contribution.

The strategy is viable but slightly too dependent on obtaining an Award opportunity.

### Patch 2.2B — Critical Acclaim
A Quality 5 product that sells 5K+ in a quarter may take a **Critical Acclaim** marker if it does not already hold Award/Bestseller.

Critical Acclaim: +1 Deal score only; it does not add Sales Appeal.

This gives premium publishers licensing/retail leverage without making Awards automatic.

# 6. KDP / Licensing Test

Storyforge produced positive cash/profit earliest and suffered the least inventory risk.

However, KDP alone has insufficient scale to win, which is intentional.

Licensing substantially improves the route but visible licence randomness could affect it too much.

### Patch 2.2C — Licence Development
DEALS fallback Business Development now lets a player choose:
- cycle one visible Licence/Retail card for $10K, OR
- pay $20K to draw two Licence cards, keep one as a private opportunity, return/discard the other according to deck rules.

Maximum one private Licence opportunity per company.

This gives licensing specialists agency without guaranteeing the best deal.

# 7. Marketing Staff Test

PPC Specialist was close to an automatic purchase for an Amazon-heavy company.

At $30K/year, using it three/four times can save $30–40K and becomes too obviously efficient.

### Patch 2.2D
PPC Specialist: $40K/year; Sponsored Ads $10K cheaper maximum **twice per year**, not once per quarter.

Marketing Manager remains $50K/year but its free operational Organic Social ability is maximum twice/year.

Email Platform remains $20K/year but reduces Email Campaign cost by $10K maximum twice/year.

Permanent marketing staff should be strategic commitments, not compulsory optimisations.

# 8. Action Pressure

MARKET competes strongly with DESIGN and PRODUCE in early years. This is desirable but can make a player feel unable to advertise a launch after spending actions creating/manufacturing it.

### Patch 2.2E — Launch Preparation
When a player uses DESIGN to create a brand-new product, they may place one **Launch Plan** marker on it by paying $10K.

When that product first becomes sale-ready, discard Launch Plan to buy one eligible paid campaign without consuming the MARKET Action. Pay the full campaign cost.

Maximum one Launch Plan per company at a time.

This rewards planning rather than simply granting free advertising.

# 9. Advertising ROI Aid

Players need to assess whether campaigns are worthwhile without difficult calculations.

Add a Player Aid reminder:

**BREAK-EVEN EXTRA SALES = Campaign Cost ÷ Profit per 1K additional sales.**

Provide examples:
- $20K campaign / $10K profit per 1K = needs ~2K extra sales.
- $40K campaign / $10K = ~4K.
- $50K / $20K = ~3K.

Players may estimate; exact accounting is not required before choosing.

# 10. Marketing Spend Visibility

All completed campaign cards/markers remain face-up until Quarter End.

Opponents therefore see how much advertising is active after Action reveal, but cannot know it before committing their own hidden Action.

This creates competitive reaction without hidden bookkeeping.

# 11. Over-Marketing Penalty

No artificial penalty is needed. The economic penalty is the campaign cost itself plus the Sales Claim/demand/stock caps.

Do NOT add customer fatigue or random advertising failure at this stage. Those mechanisms add administration/luck without improving the central decision.

# 12. Q4 Finding

Christmas advertising is very strong because Q4 demand is high. The Christmas PPC Surge card at +4 for $40K can be excellent.

### Patch 2.2F
Christmas PPC Surge becomes $50K and +4 Amazon Appeal, Q4 only.

A player with inadequate stock can still waste it.

# 13. Brand Progression Finding

Brand advancement was too easy when routine Retail/Licence successes counted equally with marketing/community building.

### Patch 2.2G
To gain +1 Brand at Annual Accounts, complete three Brand activities from at least two categories, and **at least one must be customer-facing**:
- Organic/Community
- Influencer/Reviewer
- 4K+ Website quarter
- Major Convention
- Award/Bestseller

Retail/Licence success can count as the second category but cannot alone create Brand.

# 14. Victory Timing

The model produced three companies crossing $2M in Year 5. This is desirable for the Standard Game because the finish becomes competitive rather than predetermined.

However, after-tax victory at $2M means pre-tax Profit must be substantially higher. The current tax abstraction makes the headline target tougher than a '$2M profit' player may intuitively expect.

### Patch 2.2H — Victory Definition
For clarity, the commercial box/rulebook should state:

**WIN THE RACE BY BECOMING THE FIRST COMPANY TO RECORD $2 MILLION ANNUAL AFTER-TAX PROFIT.**

Do not alternate between pre-tax and after-tax language.

# 15. Marketing Strategy Assessment

Amazon-heavy: fast scale, vulnerable to advertising waste and inventory mistakes.

Brand/Website: slowest early investment, strongest efficient mature direct-sales engine.

Premium/PR: capital intensive, high margin, benefits from Quality/Reputation/critical acclaim.

KDP/licensing: safest Cash profile and early profitability, lower natural ceiling but excellent diversification/support route.

No marketing strategy is currently an obvious automatic winner.
