# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 0.8 — Physical Play System

Purpose: turn the tested business model into a board game that can be played mainly by moving markers, cards and stock cubes rather than writing accounts.

## 1. Core Physical Design Principle

The table should show the state of every business at a glance.

Players should not need calculators for routine play. Most monetary values are rounded to $10K increments and printed on cards/player aids.

## 4. Stock Cubes

1 Stock Cube = 1,000 copies.

Recommended prototype: wooden cubes or inexpensive generic cubes in each player's colour.

The cubes themselves do not need monetary printing.

Each production batch instead receives one **Cost of Sales tile** placed beside the product/batch.

Example:
- China Standard Game batch: COST $17K / CUBE
- Europe Compact batch: COST $12K / CUBE

When one cube sells, the player takes the printed channel contribution and subtracts the Cost tile value. A player aid can show the resulting Gross Profit directly where common combinations exist.

## 5. Cost of Sales Tiles

Rather than calculate factory + freight repeatedly, issue a rounded Cost tile when production is ordered.

Prototype standard landed cost per 1K stock cube:

### China
- Card: $7K
- Compact: $10K
- Standard: $18K
- Deluxe: $30K

### Europe to UK/EU
- Card: $8K
- Compact: $12K
- Standard: $22K
- Deluxe: $37K

### USA Domestic
- Card: $9K
- Compact: $14K
- Standard: $24K
- Deluxe: $38K

Volume run discount tiles:
- 5K–9K: use DISCOUNT −10% marker / rounded batch tile
- 10K+: DISCOUNT −20%

For the physical prototype, final rounded landed-cost tables should give players the correct tile immediately.

## 6. Profit When Stock Sells

For physical products:

**Channel Contribution − Cost of Sales = Gross Profit**

Example:
UK Amazon Standard Board Game, Standard Price:
- Channel contribution: $29K per 1K
- China landed Cost of Sales: $18K
- Gross Profit: $11K per cube

Move Cash by the full $29K contribution when sold because manufacturing was already paid earlier.

Move Current-Year Profit by $11K.

This elegantly separates cash from profit.

## 7. Development and Other Expenses

When paying a development, marketing, staff, storage, interest or overhead expense:
- reduce Cash by the cost when paid
- reduce Current-Year Profit by the same amount if it is a current-year expense

Inventory manufacturing payment reduces Cash immediately but does not reduce Profit until the corresponding stock is sold.

This is the key accounting simplification discovered in Prototype 0.7.

## 8. KDP Books

KDP needs no Stock Cubes.

For every 1K KDP sales:
- Cash +$9K
- Profit +$9K

Development/marketing expenses are tracked separately.

## 9. Physical Direct-Sale Books

Physical books use Small stock cubes.

Working landed stock cost: approximately $3K per 1K books for the owner's low-cost direct-buy model.

At a typical direct contribution of $13K:
- Cash received: +$13K
- Profit from sold cube: +$10K

This makes the higher direct margin visually obvious while the player still has to fund and store inventory.

## 10. Appeal Track

Repeated arithmetic should be removed.

Each product slot has an Appeal track from 0–20.

When something changes, move the Appeal marker:
- Quality upgrade
- Price change
- Market Trend change
- Marketing
- Reputation threshold
- Award/Bestseller
- Product ageing

A small player-aid strip lists the modifiers.

Before Sales Resolution, players check/move the marker once rather than recalculate during every sale.

## 11. Sales Claim Strip

Printed beside the Appeal track:
- Appeal 0–1 → 1K
- 2–4 → 2K
- 5–7 → 3K
- 8–10 → 4K
- 11–13 → 5K
- 14+ → 6K

Players therefore read sales potential directly from the board.

## 12. Market Demand Markers

Market demand should be physical.

Example:
UK Fantasy demand 12K = 12 demand units/tokens in the Fantasy market row.

When a product sells 5K, remove/move five demand units.

The next competing product can see exactly how many customers remain.

For production economy, final components may use one marker on a numbered demand track instead of twelve individual tokens. Prototype both methods.

Recommended final direction: numbered tracks to reduce component count and setup time.

## 13. Price Markers

Each product gets a three-position marker:
- BUDGET
- STANDARD
- PREMIUM

The marker simultaneously reminds players of demand/Appeal effect and points them to the correct sales contribution column.

## 14. Quality Markers

Five-step Quality track with star icons.

Quality upgrades are deliberate investments, never random.

## 15. Product Life-Cycle Marker

Four positions:
- LAUNCH
- GROWTH
- MATURE
- DECLINE

Move at the prescribed year/quarter intervals.

## 16. Production Order Boards

When ordering:
1. choose factory
2. choose exact quantity
3. pay manufacturing/freight cash
4. place Order marker on factory capacity
5. take appropriate Cost of Sales tile
6. put stock/order cubes into the pipeline

When they arrive, move them into the chosen territory warehouse.

## 17. Action Tiles

Each player receives six reusable action tiles:
- DESIGN
- PRODUCE
- MARKET
- EXPAND
- DEALS
- FINANCE

At the start of each action round, players secretly choose one and reveal simultaneously.

The actual detailed action is then chosen within that category.

This keeps human prediction central to play.

## 18. Forecast Cards

Forecast cards use two information bands:

### Full Information
When in NOW or NEXT, exact effect is visible.

### Signal Information
When in LATER, only category may be visible, e.g.:
- FREIGHT PRESSURE
- CONSUMER BOOM
- PLATFORM CHANGE
- RETAIL OPPORTUNITY

When it moves to NEXT, flip/reveal exact values.

This preserves planning while retaining controlled uncertainty.

## 19. Player Aid

Each player gets a double-sided reference card.

Side A:
- Quarter sequence
- Appeal modifiers
- Sales Claim table
- action choices

Side B:
- Channel contribution table
- landed Cost of Sales table
- annual overhead/tax summary

Goal: ordinary turns should not require opening the rulebook.

Every future component addition must be checked against:

## 24. Physical Playtest Success Criteria

Prototype is successful if:
1. Players understand Cash vs Profit.
2. Routine sales resolve in under 60 seconds per contested genre.
3. Players can forecast manufacturing needs without a calculator.
4. Players discuss competitors' choices.
5. Players feel responsible for their financial results.
6. No dominant strategy is obvious after a few games.
7. The board state is readable from across the table.
8. Most rulebook lookups disappear after the first year.
