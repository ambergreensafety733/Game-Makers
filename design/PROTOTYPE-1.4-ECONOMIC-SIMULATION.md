# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 1.4 — Economic Simulation & Balance Pass 1

Purpose: test whether different player strategies can build from $500K starting Cash toward the $2M annual after-tax Profit target under the two-actions-per-quarter system.

This is a mathematical design simulation, not a claim that physical playtesting is complete.

# 1. Simulation Principles

The model assumes competent strategic play, no arbitrary dice outcomes, visible market information, reasonable competition and occasional contested resources.

Seven archetypes are tested:
1. Premium Board Games
2. China Volume / Amazon
3. Direct Website
4. Books / KDP
5. Licensing
6. European Fast Manufacturing
7. Diversified Publisher

Values are rounded to $10K–$50K bands because the tabletop game should not require accounting precision.

# 2. Main Finding

The original Prototype 1.2 Year 5–6 target is too slow for a Standard Game capped at five years if the box is to promise roughly 2–3 hours.

Recommended Standard Game economic arc:
- Year 1: setup / first products
- Year 2: profitable growth
- Year 3: portfolio expansion
- Year 4: international scale
- Year 5: realistic $2M challenge

The $2M target should normally be reachable by one or more strong players during Year 5, but not automatically.

# 3. Revised Strong-Company After-Tax Profit Target

- Year 1: −$100K to +$100K
- Year 2: $150K–$400K
- Year 3: $450K–$850K
- Year 4: $900K–$1.5M
- Year 5: $1.5M–$2.4M+

Exceptional Year 4 wins should be possible but rare and require strong positioning rather than a random windfall.

# 4. Strategy A — Premium Board Games

Typical decisions:
- invest in Quality 4–5
- Standard/Deluxe formats
- Premium pricing when Reputation supports it
- China for planned large runs
- Europe/USA for emergency replenishment
- pursue Awards

Indicative after-tax path:
- Y1: −$100K
- Y2: $180K
- Y3: $520K
- Y4: $1.05M
- Y5: $1.85M–$2.25M

Strengths:
- high margin per successful unit
- Awards/Reputation synergy
- strong late-game products

Risks:
- expensive development
- heavy inventory
- freight/storage
- premium price reduces Appeal

Balance conclusion: viable but should not dominate without Quality/Reputation investment.

# 5. Strategy B — China Volume / Amazon

Typical decisions:
- Card/Compact/Standard products
- 10K+ runs for lower Cost of Sales
- Amazon reach
- Budget/Standard pricing
- Bestseller pursuit

Indicative path:
- Y1: $0–$50K
- Y2: $250K
- Y3: $650K
- Y4: $1.15M
- Y5: $1.7M–$2.2M

Strengths:
- best large-run economics
- strong volume
- Bestseller potential

Risks:
- long lead times
- cash tied up in stock
- storage
- forecast freight exposure
- stock can arrive after demand peak

Balance conclusion: strong but strategically exposed. China must remain cheapest without becoming the automatic factory choice.

# 6. Strategy C — Direct Website

Typical decisions:
- early Website investment
- community marketing
- higher-margin direct sales
- fewer Amazon fees
- physical books/Compact games particularly attractive

Indicative path:
- Y1: −$50K–$50K
- Y2: $200K
- Y3: $600K
- Y4: $1.2M
- Y5: $1.9M–$2.35M

Strengths:
- high unit margins
- direct customer base

Risks:
- Website capacity must be built
- marketing required to create demand
- early investment produces no automatic customers

Balance conclusion: viable late-game contender. Website Level 4 must not become unlimited; 6K/product/quarter remains sensible initial cap.

# 7. Strategy D — Books / KDP

Typical decisions:
- low development costs
- KDP inventory-free sales
- cheap physical books for Website/Etsy
- translations

Indicative path if almost pure books:
- Y1: $50K
- Y2: $180K
- Y3: $350K
- Y4: $600K
- Y5: $850K–$1.2M

Conclusion: books are intentionally safer but a pure book strategy currently struggles to win the $2M race.

Recommended adjustment: keep books as a strong cash-flow/portfolio strategy rather than inflate them unrealistically. Storyforge should be encouraged to use book profits to expand into licensed/physical games or multiple languages.

This preserves the real strategic distinction: lower risk, lower ceiling.

# 8. Strategy E — Licensing

Typical decisions:
- develop attractive IP
- invest in Quality/Reputation
- secure multiple territories
- accept lower upside in return for no inventory exposure

Indicative path:
- Y1: −$50K
- Y2: $150K
- Y3: $500K
- Y4: $1.0M
- Y5: $1.6M–$2.1M

Balance concern: licence advances can become too powerful if they are pure profit.

Rule clarification:
- Licence advance increases Cash and Profit when received.
- Royalty income increases Cash and Profit.
- but each product may hold only a limited number of active territory licences and offers have eligibility/term constraints.

Recommended cap for prototype: maximum 3 active external territory licences per product.

# 9. Strategy F — European Fast Manufacturing

Typical decisions:
- smaller/mid-size replenishment
- react to visible forecasts
- avoid stockouts
- pay higher unit cost for speed

Indicative path:
- Y1: $0
- Y2: $220K
- Y3: $560K
- Y4: $1.05M
- Y5: $1.65M–$2.1M

Strengths:
- responsiveness
- lower inventory exposure

Risks:
- weaker unit economics than China

Balance conclusion: strategically viable, particularly when demand information is valuable.

# 10. Strategy G — Diversified Publisher

Typical decisions:
- mix Amazon + Website
- one or two books
- selective licences
- China for predictable products
- Europe for replenishment
- several genres

Indicative path:
- Y1: −$50K–$50K
- Y2: $250K
- Y3: $700K
- Y4: $1.3M
- Y5: $2.0M–$2.5M

This currently looks like the strongest robust strategy, which is acceptable if diversification requires more planning/actions and does not simply cherry-pick every advantage.

# 11. Important Balance Principle — Specialisation vs Diversification

Specialists should be more efficient inside their niche.
Diversified companies should be more resilient.

To prevent diversification becoming automatically best, introduce **Portfolio Management Overhead**:
- 1–2 active physical products: no extra overhead
- 3–4 active physical products: +$30K/year
- 5–6 active physical products: +$70K/year

Books/KDP-only titles count as half for this threshold; two book titles count as one physical-product equivalent.

This makes portfolio size a decision.

# 12. Territory Management Overhead

International scale also creates cost:
- UK only: no additional territory overhead
- UK + one foreign territory: +$30K/year
- UK + EU + USA: +$70K/year

This prevents international expansion being an automatic yes.

# 13. Revised Company Scale Thresholds

Use annual Revenue measured on a printed Revenue/Scale track rather than detailed bookkeeping.

Startup: default.

Growing:
- $750K annual Revenue
- 2 active products

Established:
- $1.75M annual Revenue
- Reputation 4+

International:
- $3.5M annual Revenue
- active in 2 territories

Global:
- $5.5M annual Revenue
- active in all 3 territories
- Reputation 7+

Scale benefits remain the limited Executive Actions from Prototype 1.3.

# 14. Revenue Tracking Simplification

A separate exact Revenue account would add administration.

Recommended: Company Board has a Revenue track in $100K steps.

Whenever Cash is received from sales/licensing/retail, move Revenue by the rounded amount.

Revenue resets at Annual Accounts after Scale qualification is checked.

This adds one marker but avoids written calculations.

# 15. Profit Target and Tax

Victory remains **$2M annual after-tax Profit**.

Simplified tax should not dominate play.

Recommended revised tax:
- first $250K positive annual profit: 20%
- amount above $250K: 25%

For physical play, Annual Accounts player aid will provide a simple tax lookup table in $100K bands so no multiplication is required.

# 16. Cash-Flow Stress Test

Starting Cash of $500K is sufficient for several openings but not for unrestricted premium expansion.

Examples:
- expensive Deluxe development + large China order can consume most starting liquidity
- Card/Compact opening leaves more flexibility
- KDP creates low-risk cash
- loans become strategically relevant rather than mandatory

Conclusion: retain $500K starting Cash for next physical test.

# 17. Inventory Stress Test

A 10K China run creates attractive Cost of Sales but 10 stock cubes can represent a substantial fraction of starting capital.

Storage rule needs teeth without becoming bookkeeping.

Recommended annual excess-stock charge:
- first 5K unsold physical stock across company: free base capacity
- 6K–10K: $20K
- 11K–20K: $50K
- 21K+: $100K

Territory/warehouse upgrades can increase free capacity.

# 18. Product Ageing

To stop old successful products producing effortless profit forever:
- Launch: +1
- Growth: 0
- Mature: −1
- Decline: −2

Recommended timing:
- launch year: Launch/Growth
- following full year: Growth
- next year: Mature
- next year: Decline

A New Edition action can return a Mature/Decline product to Growth for a development cost.

# 19. Market Demand Stress Test

Prototype 1.2 market-demand tracks support meaningful competition early, but late-game companies need access to more demand through:
- EU
- USA
- Website growth
- retail contracts
- licences

Do NOT simply increase UK demand every year. Geographic/channel expansion should be how companies scale.

# 20. Winning Company Shape

A typical $2M winner should look something like:
- 4–6 meaningful active products
- at least 2 territories or major licensing network
- developed Website and/or strong Amazon presence
- Reputation 5+
- controlled but significant inventory
- several marketing/deal investments
- strong Cash position but not necessarily the most Cash

This should feel like a successful international games publisher rather than a single lucky bestseller.

# 21. Strategy Balance Summary

Current provisional Year-5 winning potential:
- Premium: HIGH
- China/Amazon: HIGH
- Website: HIGH
- Books-only: LOW/MEDIUM but safe
- Licensing: HIGH with constraints
- Europe: MEDIUM/HIGH
- Diversified: HIGH, checked by portfolio overhead

No single factory/channel should be mandatory.

# 22. Luck Audit

No core simulated strategy depends on dice.

Controlled uncertainty remains in future forecasts/card-market availability, but players see enough information to plan and can change strategy.

Most financial failure modes remain decision-based:
- overproduction
- stockout
- poor price
- wrong genre
- overexpansion
- excessive overhead
- slow manufacturing choice
- underinvestment in marketing/channel capacity
- weak cash management
