# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 1.3 — Action Economy & Player Interaction

Goal: make the game highly strategic and interactive without allowing four-player analysis to push playing time beyond the intended 2–3 hour range.

# 1. Core Timing Decision

Each quarter has **two Business Rounds**.

In each Business Round every player secretly selects ONE Action tile, then all reveal simultaneously.

Therefore a Startup company normally receives:
- 2 main actions per quarter
- 8 main actions per year

This replaces the earlier one-action-per-quarter interpretation, which is too restrictive for meaningful business development.

# 2. Why Two Actions Per Quarter

A player needs enough capacity to combine decisions such as:
- Design + Market
- Produce + Expand
- Market + Deals
- Produce + Finance

but cannot do everything.

Choosing to spend one of only two quarterly actions on a new Website, a large production run or a licence pursuit creates a real opportunity cost.

# 3. Quarter Structure

1. FORECAST — advance/reveal business conditions.
2. MARKET — update trends/demand.
3. BUSINESS ROUND A — secret Action selection, reveal, resolve.
4. BUSINESS ROUND B — secret Action selection, reveal, resolve.
5. ALLOCATION — move/allocate available stock to territories/channels.
6. SALES — resolve competing products.
7. FINANCE — update Cash/Profit and immediate expenses.
8. PIPELINE — advance production/shipping.
9. QUARTER END — update temporary effects.

Q4 then proceeds to Annual Accounts.

# 4. Action Tiles

Each player owns:
- DESIGN
- PRODUCE
- MARKET
- EXPAND
- DEALS
- FINANCE

A player **may not use the same Action category twice in the same quarter** unless a specific company/scale effect permits it.

This prevents repetitive optimisation such as producing twice every quarter and forces broader business planning.

# 5. Simultaneous Planning

Players select their tile at the same time.

No player waits while another spends several minutes deciding.

Recommended physical rule: once all players have placed a face-down tile, reveal together.

Target decision window during playtests: approximately 60–90 seconds per Business Round. No formal sand timer in the base game initially; add one only if testing shows it is necessary.

# 6. Initiative

Use a rotating **First Company** marker.

At the beginning of each quarter, First Company passes clockwise.

Most independent actions resolve simultaneously. Initiative matters only when players compete for a limited shared resource.

This avoids making turn order overly powerful.

# 7. Contested Concepts

If two or more DESIGN players want the same face-up Concept:

1. Each secretly places a bid from $0K/$10K/$20K/$30K using bid tokens or closed hand.
2. Reveal simultaneously.
3. Highest bid pays and takes the Concept.
4. Tie: lower current annual Profit gets priority; then rotating initiative.

Reason: desirable concepts become strategically contested without dice.

Players may instead choose another available Concept without bidding if they do not want the contest.

# 8. Contested Factory Capacity

Factories have limited visible quarterly capacity.

Players choosing PRODUCE simultaneously place intended order markers face-down, then reveal.

If total requested capacity fits, all orders proceed.

If demand exceeds factory capacity:
1. players may voluntarily reduce quantities
2. remaining conflict goes to a sealed **Priority Premium** bid
3. highest premium receives capacity first
4. premium becomes additional production Cash cost
5. losing player can choose another available factory or cancel the production action without paying manufacturing cost

This makes factory scarcity a business decision, not random denial.

# 9. Contested Licences

Licence cards show eligibility and economics.

If multiple DEALS players pursue one licence:
1. compare mandatory eligibility
2. compare Quality
3. compare Reputation
4. if tied, sealed bid accepting a reduced advance
5. player willing to accept the lowest advance wins

This simulates publishers competing commercially for rights.

# 10. Contested Retail Contracts

If multiple eligible players want the same retailer:
1. retailer's printed Priority criteria decide first — e.g. Quality, price, Reputation, stock availability
2. if still tied, players may offer a retailer discount in printed $10K steps
3. best qualifying offer wins

Retail contracts therefore reward the product/business characteristics the retailer actually values.

# 11. Marketing Interaction

Marketing is not purely solitaire.

A genre with three or more actively marketed competing products receives Saturation pressure.

Players can see competitors' marketing after reveal and may respond in Business Round B.

Example:
Round A: Dragon Forge reveals MARKET on Fantasy.
Tiny Giant had chosen PRODUCE.
Round B: Tiny Giant can now decide whether to MARKET its competing Fantasy product, change focus, or invest elsewhere.

This creates reaction and counterplay within the same quarter.

# 12. Price Interaction

Prices are set during Allocation before Sales and are simultaneously revealed/locked for the quarter.

Players use face-down Budget/Standard/Premium markers, then reveal together.

This prevents a player simply waiting to see every competitor's price before choosing theirs.

Price therefore becomes a prediction game:
- Budget gains Appeal/demand but sacrifices margin.
- Premium sacrifices Appeal but increases margin.
- Standard balances both.

# 13. Inventory Allocation Interaction

Before Sales, each player secretly allocates available inventory among opened channels/territories using stock cubes behind a small screen or face-down allocation board, then reveals.

For the low-art prototype, players can write/mark allocations secretly on a reusable sheet if screens are not yet available.

Final manufacturing decision on screens should wait for playtesting because four screens add cost/weight.

# 14. Sales Interaction

Once prices and allocations reveal, Appeal is public and sales resolve quickly.

Players should immediately see why a competitor won demand:
- stronger product
- lower price
- better marketing
- stronger reputation
- better channel
- available stock

The outcome is deterministic from those choices.

# 15. Company Scale — Revised Extra Actions

To prevent runaway leaders, replace the earlier broad extra-action benefits with **Executive Actions**.

## Startup
2 main actions/quarter.

## Growing
Once per YEAR: one Executive Action.

## Established
Once per HALF-YEAR: one Executive Action (max 2/year).

## International
Once per QUARTER: one Executive Action.

## Global
Once per quarter one Executive Action; additionally one extra Executive Action in Q4.

Executive Actions are intentionally narrower than main actions.

# 16. Executive Action Menu

An Executive Action may do ONE:
- place/replenish an existing product production order up to 5K
- buy one Marketing action costing up to $30K
- repay/take standard finance
- make one Website/warehouse upgrade
- pursue one already-visible eligible deal

It may NOT:
- DESIGN a brand-new product
- take a major custom strategic action
- repeat another Executive Action that quarter

This gives large companies operational capacity without doubling strategic turns.

# 17. Two-Player Competition Rule

With only two players, shared markets can otherwise feel too open.

Use **Neutral Competitor Products** in two genres each year.

Neutral competitors:
- occupy market demand
- have printed Appeal and price
- do not take turns
- change only through Market Outlook cards

Players can deliberately avoid or challenge them.

This maintains competitive pressure without an automated opponent requiring administration.

# 18. Three/Four-Player Rule

Neutral competitors are not normally used.

Real players provide sufficient competition for:
- concepts
- factory capacity
- licences
- retail
- genre demand
- price positioning

# 19. Anti-Analysis-Paralysis Design

The game should limit calculation rather than limit strategy.

Rules:
- all common financial outcomes printed on player aids
- Appeal track maintained continuously
- Sales Claim read directly from Appeal
- Cost of Sales attached to batch
- only 5 Concepts/3 Licences/3 Retail offers visible at once
- only two Business Round decisions per quarter
- prices use three choices rather than arbitrary dollar amounts
- production quantities use 1K increments but recommended quick-order bands of 3K/5K/8K/10K are printed

# 20. Target Timing

Experienced group target:

Per quarter:
- Forecast/Market: 2 min
- Business Round A: 3–4 min
- Business Round B: 3–4 min
- Allocation/Price: 2–3 min
- Sales/Finance/Pipeline: 3–4 min

Target quarter: **13–17 minutes**.

Target year including Accounts: **55–70 minutes in Year 1 during learning**, falling toward 35–45 minutes once experienced and simultaneous play improves.

A six-year game at full literal timing would exceed target, so game length needs a further structural solution.

# 21. Important Timing Finding

The 6-year maximum and 2–3 hour target conflict if every year contains eight full main actions per player.

Recommended solution for next balance test:

### Standard Game
Maximum **5 years**, with $2M normally reachable in Year 4–5 through stronger scale economics.

### First Game / Short Game
Maximum **3 years**, victory by highest after-tax annual Profit at end Year 3; $2M instant win remains possible.

### Epic Game
Maximum **6 years** for players who want the full business simulation.

This is preferable to stripping strategic decisions from every quarter simply to meet a box play-time claim.

# 22. Catch-Up Philosophy

No artificial cash handout to trailing players.

Natural recovery mechanisms should instead include:
- cheaper unclaimed concepts
- less crowded genres
- licensing without inventory
- KDP/books
- European short production
- budget pricing
- finance
- competitors creating openings through stockouts

A losing company should recover by changing strategy.

# 23. Bankruptcy

A player is not immediately eliminated at $0 Cash.

If unable to meet a required payment, they may:
1. take available loan finance
2. liquidate stock at a printed clearance value
3. sell/relinquish a product right for a fixed recovery value

If still unable to meet mandatory obligations, company enters Administration.

Administration rule for prototype: receive $200K rescue finance, lose 2 Reputation, discard one product, and incur a $50K restructuring cost against annual Profit. A company in Administration cannot win that same year.

This keeps everyone playing while making failure painful.

# 24. Player Interaction Success Criteria

A playtest succeeds if players regularly say/thought-process things like:
- "They're going into Fantasy, so I'll choose Family."
- "If I wait until next quarter China will be full."
- "They'll probably price Budget, so Premium may be a mistake."
- "I need that licence before their Reputation rises."
- "They only have 2K stock left; I can take the remaining Christmas demand."

If players mostly stare only at their own Company Boards, interaction needs strengthening.

# 25. Prototype 1.4 Next

Build the **first full economic simulation model** around the revised two-action-per-quarter system.

Test at least these strategy archetypes across Years 1–5:
- premium board games
- China volume/Amazon
- direct Website
- books/KDP
- licensing
- European fast manufacturing
- diversified portfolio

Measure:
- annual revenue
- gross profit
- after-tax profit
- cash
- inventory
- actions used
- stockouts
- market share
- year reaching $2M

Use results to rebalance demand, margins, scale thresholds and victory timing before final printable artwork.
