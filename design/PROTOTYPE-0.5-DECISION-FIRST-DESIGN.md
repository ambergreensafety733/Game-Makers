# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 0.5 — Decision-First, Low-Luck Design

## Core Design Commitment

**GAME MAKERS must be won primarily by the quality of the players' business decisions, not by lucky card draws or dice rolls.**

Target design balance:
- approximately **80–90% player decision / planning / interaction**
- approximately **10–20% controlled market uncertainty**
- **no dice required for the core game**
- no random event should by itself create or destroy a winning company

The player should normally be able to see risks before committing money.

# 1. Replace Random Event Draws with a Forecast System

Instead of drawing a surprise Event every quarter, reveal a **Business Forecast Row** of three face-up cards.

Example:
- CURRENT QUARTER: Container Shortage
- NEXT QUARTER: Amazon Promotion Week
- QUARTER AFTER: Christmas Boom

At the end of a quarter, slide cards left and reveal one new forecast at the far end.

Players therefore know much of what is coming and can plan around it.

Example decision:
A player sees China freight will rise next quarter. They can order now, switch to Europe, delay production, or accept the extra cost.

This converts randomness into strategic information.

# 2. Annual Market Outlook Is Public

At the beginning of each year reveal the Market Outlook before players commit to major product development.

Example:
- Fantasy: HOT
- Family: NORMAL
- Party: BOOM
- Strategy: LOW

Players can choose whether to follow the market, anticipate competitors following it, or deliberately enter an unfashionable category where competition may be weaker.

# 3. Market Trends Should Be Influenceable

Players are not passive victims of trends.

Possible actions:
- $30K Trend Campaign: move one genre one step toward HOT for your own product this quarter.
- Convention Showcase: improve awareness of a genre/product.
- Bestseller/Award: permanently strengthen that product.
- Multiple competing launches in the same genre create Saturation.

Thus players help create the market rather than merely drawing it.

# 4. No Random Shipping Delay Roll

Remove dice/random resolution from Shipping Delay.

Replacement:
When a Shipping Delay forecast reaches Current Quarter, every affected player chooses one:
1. Accept a one-quarter delay.
2. Pay $30K priority freight to arrive normally.
3. Divert the shipment to another market for $20K.
4. If eligible, source an emergency local run at higher cost.

The problem is fixed; the response is strategic.

# 5. Licence Offers Become a Visible Market

Do not normally deal random licence cards secretly.

Reveal **3 face-up Licence Offers** in a common Licence Market.

Players can spend a Negotiate Licence action to claim an eligible offer.

If multiple players want the same offer, priority is determined by:
1. Product eligibility/Quality.
2. Reputation.
3. If still tied, player willing to accept a reduced advance (sealed simultaneous bid).

After an offer is taken, refill the market.

This creates competition and informed decisions.

# 6. Factory Market Is Fully Visible

All factory cards, capacity, prices, lead times and specialties are visible.

Players choose where to manufacture.

There is no random factory allocation.

Factory capacity is limited. Turn/action timing therefore matters.

A player can deliberately reserve capacity early but sacrifices an action that could have been used elsewhere.

# 7. Simultaneous Business Planning

At the beginning of each quarter each player secretly selects their first Business Action using an Action Tile.

Suggested action categories:
- DESIGN
- PRODUCE
- MARKET
- EXPAND
- DEALS
- FINANCE

Reveal simultaneously.

Players then resolve actions in initiative order.

Why this matters:
Players must predict what competitors are likely to do.

Example:
Only 10 China factory capacity remains. Two players may both choose PRODUCE. One may have priority because they invested in Operations or took an earlier reservation action.

# 8. Turn Order Must Be Strategic

Avoid a simple permanent clockwise advantage.

At the end of each quarter, the player with the **lowest annual profit** chooses their position in next quarter's initiative order first, then the next-lowest, etc.

Alternative under testing: players bid small amounts of cash for initiative.

This gives trailing companies tactical opportunity without artificial catch-up money.

# 9. Competitor Demand Is Deterministic

When products compete in the same genre/market, customer demand is divided using visible Appeal.

Working Appeal:

**Quality + Marketing + Reputation + Trend Fit + Price Appeal + Awards/Bestseller**

Highest Appeal receives first access to available demand, but lower-ranked products can still sell according to remaining market capacity.

No sales dice.

If two products tie, the cheaper product wins the tie; if prices also tie, higher Reputation wins; if still tied, they split demand.

# 10. Market Capacity

Each market/genre has visible quarterly customer capacity.

Example UK Fantasy Q4:
- Market capacity: 12K

Dragon Forge product Appeal 10
Silver Owl product Appeal 8
MeepleWorks product Appeal 7

Players know there are only 12K potential sales before committing marketing and inventory.

This makes competitor observation important.

# 11. Price Is a Real Competitive Weapon

Players may change Budget/Standard/Premium positioning.

A Budget competitor can take demand from a Premium competitor, but earns less per sale.

A Premium company can protect margin through Quality, Reputation and Awards.

Players should be able to see competitors' current prices before making the next quarter's decision.

# 12. Manufacturing Quantity Is Central

No card determines how many copies are made.

The player chooses the exact production batch permitted by factory capacity:
1K, 2K, 3K ... 10K+.

Large runs gain volume discounts but:
- consume cash
- consume factory capacity
- create storage exposure
- risk obsolete/declining stock

Small runs:
- preserve cash
- reduce storage risk
- have worse unit economics
- increase stockout risk
- may require expensive emergency manufacture

This should be one of the most important skill tests in the game.

# 13. Product Development Is Chosen, Not Random

Game Concept cards will be displayed in a **Concept Market** of 5 face-up opportunities.

A player taking Develop chooses one available concept and immediately refills the market.

A player may alternatively commission a Custom Concept for +$20K and choose a genre, but receives Base Appeal 3 rather than potentially stronger specialist concepts.

This prevents a player losing because they drew poor concepts.

# 14. Quality Is an Investment Decision

Quality improvements are purchased deliberately.

Players choose between:
- launch cheaply/quickly
- invest in artwork
- invest in playtesting
- premium components
- stronger pre-launch marketing

Higher Quality should help but not guarantee success.

# 15. Marketing Is Mostly Deterministic

Standard marketing actions have known effects.

Examples:
- Amazon Ads $20K = +2 Amazon Appeal/Demand
- Social Campaign $20K = +1 broad Demand
- Community Building $30K = improve Website channel
- Convention $30K = licensing/reputation opportunity

Influencer marketing should be redesigned away from a random +1 to +4 result.

Replacement:
Choose one of three known influencer tiers:
- Micro Influencers: $10K, +1 Demand
- Established Creator: $30K, +2 Demand
- Major Campaign: $60K, +4 Demand

No random result.

# 16. Retail Contracts Are Face-Up

Maintain 3 face-up Retail Contracts.

Players can plan production around them and compete to secure them.

Contracts expire after a stated number of quarters if nobody takes them, so waiting has consequences.

# 17. Controlled Uncertainty Remains

The game still needs changing conditions so it is not solvable like a spreadsheet.

Uncertainty comes from:
- future forecast cards beyond the visible horizon
- other players' simultaneous decisions
- competitors changing prices
- competitors entering markets
- factory capacity competition
- licence/retail competition
- evolving product life cycles

This is **uncertainty**, not arbitrary luck.

# 18. Information Horizon

Recommended default:
- Current quarter fully known.
- Next quarter fully forecast.
- Following quarter partially forecast.
- Annual Market Outlook known for the whole year.

Advanced players can therefore plan 2–4 quarters ahead while still adapting.

# 19. Remove or Rewrite High-Luck Cards

Prototype 0.4 cards requiring redesign:

- Viral Game Video: no longer randomly awarded. Becomes a purchasable/competitive media opportunity.
- Influencer Campaign: fixed tier effects.
- Shipping Delay: fixed choices, no roll.
- Bestseller Buzz: Bestseller earned by performance, not arbitrary selection.
- Industry Growth: forecast in advance.
- Publisher Acquisition Offer: face-up finance opportunity rather than random rescue.

# 20. Performance-Based Bestseller

A product earns BESTSELLER when it meets a visible sales threshold, currently 8K copies in one quarter.

No random Bestseller awards.

# 21. Performance-Based Awards

Awards use a visible competition score:

**Quality + Reputation + Campaign Investment + Sales Achievement**

Eligible players decide whether entering is worth the campaign cost.

Highest score wins. Ties favour higher Quality, then sales.

No die roll.

# 22. Finance Decisions

Loans are always available under known terms, subject to borrowing limits.

Players decide:
- preserve equity/cash
- borrow to manufacture a large run
- invest in USA expansion
- build website
- accept licence advance
- reduce inventory

Cash crises should normally result from decisions, not random penalties.

# 23. Tax and VAT

Tax remains deterministic and pre-calculated/simplified.

There are no random tax-audit cards in the standard game.

# 24. Player Skill Should Be Rewarded In These Areas

A strong player should win by doing several of these better than competitors:

1. Reading market demand.
2. Forecasting competitors.
3. Choosing product portfolio.
4. Choosing price.
5. Choosing production quantity.
6. Timing manufacturing orders.
7. Managing cash flow.
8. Choosing manufacturing region.
9. Allocating marketing efficiently.
10. Building profitable sales channels.
11. Deciding when to license versus self-publish.
12. Managing stock across UK/EU/USA.
13. Controlling overhead growth.
14. Exploiting trends without entering overcrowded markets.
15. Planning Christmas stock early.

# 25. Design Test Standard

During playtesting ask:

**When a player loses money, can they usually identify the decision that caused it?**

If the answer is 'I drew an unlucky card', redesign that mechanic.

If the answer is 'I ordered too much, priced badly, entered the wrong market, mistimed production, overspent on overhead, or failed to anticipate my competitor', the game is working as intended.

# 26. Prototype 0.5 Revised Table Layout

Visible common areas:
- 5 face-up Game Concepts
- 3 face-up Licence Offers
- 3 face-up Retail Contracts
- all available Factories and remaining capacity
- Annual Market Outlook
- Current Event/Business Condition
- Next Quarter Forecast
- Partial Later Forecast
- public prices and product Appeal

Hidden information is mainly:
- each player's next planned action
- optional competitive bids

# 27. Luck Target

The finished game should aim for **low luck / high agency**.

Random setup creates replayability, but once information is revealed, players make informed choices.

The strongest player should not win every game because opponents can outthink and compete with them, but repeated good decision-making should clearly improve the chance of winning.
