# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 1.0 — Consolidated Core Ruleset

Prototype 1.0 is the first complete baseline ruleset. It supersedes conflicting earlier prototype rules.

## 1. Aim

Each player controls an independent games publisher. Design products, choose manufacturers, manage stock and cash, set prices, market products, expand internationally, sell direct or through platforms, secure retail and licensing deals, and build a profitable portfolio.

The primary victory target is to become the first company to record **$2,000,000 annual after-tax profit**.

The game is designed so success comes predominantly from player decisions, planning and competitive interaction rather than luck.

## 2. Players and Length

- 2–4 players
- Target play time after learning: 120–180 minutes
- Game is played in Business Years.
- Each year contains Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4/Christmas and Annual Accounts.
- Working maximum: 6 years.

If nobody reaches $2M annual after-tax profit by the end of Year 6, highest Year-6 after-tax profit wins. Tie: higher cash; then higher Reputation.

## 3. Starting Company

Each player begins with:
- $500K Cash
- $0 current-year Profit
- Reputation 1
- Website Level 0
- Amazon UK access
- no loans
- no staff
- no products
- standard warehouse capacity

Players choose a company name from the company roster or create their own.

## 4. Setup

1. Set Year to 1 and Quarter to Q1.
2. Place Market Outlook for Year 1 face-up.
3. Establish genre demand.
4. Place forecast cards into NOW/NEXT/LATER SIGNAL.
5. Reveal 5 Concept cards.
6. Reveal 3 Licence Offers.
7. Reveal 3 Retail/Opportunity cards.
8. Display available Factory cards and capacity.
9. Give each player a Company Board, markers, stock pieces and six Action tiles.
10. Set all starting tracks.

## 5. Core Action Tiles

Each player has:
- DESIGN
- PRODUCE
- MARKET
- EXPAND
- DEALS
- FINANCE

Players secretly select an action category and reveal simultaneously. Detailed choices are then declared and resolved.

## 6. Quarter Sequence

Each quarter:

### Phase 1 — Forecast
Advance NOW/NEXT/LATER. Reveal the exact effect when required and add a new Later Signal.

### Phase 2 — Market Update
Apply current known market/trend effects.

### Phase 3 — Planning
Players inspect public prices, products, stock, factory capacity, licences and contracts, then secretly select Action tiles.

### Phase 4 — Reveal
Reveal simultaneously.

### Phase 5 — Business Actions
Resolve actions in initiative order.

### Phase 6 — Inventory Allocation
Allocate stock to territories/channels.

### Phase 7 — Sales
Resolve market demand by genre and Appeal.

### Phase 8 — Finance
Move Cash and Profit markers for sales/current expenses.

### Phase 9 — Advance
Move production/shipping pipelines and product life-cycle markers where required.

After Q4 proceed to Annual Accounts.

## 7. DESIGN

Choose one face-up Concept and pay its development cost. Refill the market.

Alternatively commission a Custom Concept for +$20K; choose genre, Base Appeal 3.

Products may receive deliberate Quality investment in artwork, development/playtesting or components according to upgrade costs.

Quality is never determined by dice.

## 8. PRODUCE

Choose:
- product
- factory
- quantity in 1K units
- destination/route where required

Pay manufacturing and freight from Cash immediately. Take/record the batch's Cost of Sales value. Put production cubes/markers into the shipping pipeline.

Large orders receive volume economics according to current factory/rules.

Unsold inventory ties up Cash but is not charged to Profit until sold.

## 9. MARKET

Choose known-effect marketing actions. Core examples:
- Amazon Ads: $20K, +2 Amazon Appeal/Demand for one product/quarter.
- Social Campaign: $20K, +1 broad Demand/Appeal effect.
- Micro Influencers: $10K, +1.
- Established Creator: $30K, +2.
- Major Campaign: $60K, +4.
- Community Building: $30K, improves Website capacity according to current track rules.

No random influencer result.

## 10. EXPAND

Typical choices:
- open/upgrade Website
- open Etsy
- establish EU market
- establish USA market
- localise product/language
- expand warehouse capability

Prototype costs follow current player-aid tables.

## 11. DEALS

Use this action to claim/negotiate visible:
- Licence Offers
- Retail Contracts
- selected business opportunities

If several players want the same Licence, compare eligibility/Quality, Reputation, then use a sealed reduced-advance bid if still tied.

## 12. FINANCE

Typical choices:
- take standard $250K business loan
- repay loan
- restructure cash position
- use eligible visible finance opportunity

Standard loan interest: $30K per year while outstanding.

## 13. Appeal

Product Appeal is visible and maintained on the Company Board.

Appeal = Base Appeal + Quality + Reputation + Trend + Price + Marketing + Channel + Awards/Bestseller − Saturation + applicable product effects.

Core modifiers:

Quality:
- Q1 +0
- Q2 +1
- Q3 +2
- Q4 +3
- Q5 +4

Reputation:
- 0–2 +0
- 3–5 +1
- 6–8 +2
- 9–10 +3

Trend:
- Low −2
- Normal 0
- Hot +2
- Boom +4

Price:
- Budget +2
- Standard 0
- Premium −2

Amazon reach: +2.

Bestseller: +1.
Award: +1.

Saturation:
- 1–2 competing products: 0
- 3: −1 each
- 4+: −2 each

## 14. Sales Claim

Appeal determines maximum natural quarterly claim:
- 0–1: 1K
- 2–4: 2K
- 5–7: 3K
- 8–10: 4K
- 11–13: 5K
- 14+: 6K

Sales remain limited by available market demand, stock and channel capacity.

## 15. Competitive Sales

For each territory/genre:
1. establish available demand
2. rank eligible products by Appeal
3. highest Appeal sells first up to Sales Claim/stock/capacity
4. remove that demand
5. continue down ranking

Tie breakers:
1. lower consumer price
2. higher Quality
3. higher Reputation
4. split if still tied

No sales dice.

## 16. Channels

### Amazon
High reach and volume; medium margin.

### Website
Higher margin; limited capacity until developed.
- L1 max 1K/product/quarter
- L2 2K
- L3 4K
- L4 6K

### Etsy
Higher-margin niche channel with normally lower capacity.

### Retail
Visible contracts exchange margin for bulk movement/reach.

### Licensing
Advance/royalty income without publisher inventory risk.

### KDP
Eligible books: working value $9K Cash and Profit per 1K sales, no inventory.

## 17. Cash and Profit

These are deliberately separate.

### Inventory Purchase
When stock is manufactured:
- Cash falls immediately.
- Profit does NOT fall for the unsold inventory.

### Physical Sale
When 1K sells:
- increase Cash by channel contribution
- increase Profit by channel contribution minus that stock cube/batch Cost of Sales

### Current Expenses
Development, marketing, overhead, salaries, interest and similar expenses normally reduce both Cash and current-year Profit when incurred.

## 18. Stockouts

If demand exceeds available stock, sell all available stock and take SOLD OUT marker where applicable. Remaining customers can buy competing products.

A stockout therefore represents both successful demand and lost opportunity.

## 19. Inventory Risk

Unsold stock:
- occupies warehouse space
- keeps Cash tied up
- may incur storage costs
- may suffer from product ageing/decline

Large orders gain better unit economics but create greater exposure.

## 20. Product Life Cycle

Products move through:
- Launch
- Growth
- Mature
- Decline

Working modifiers:
- Launch: +1 where a proper Launch action/effect applies
- Growth: 0
- Mature: −1
- Decline: −2

Major relaunch/new edition rules may temporarily improve stage later.

## 21. Bestseller

Working qualification: sell at least 8K copies in one quarter across qualifying routes or meet specified performance condition.

Effect:
- +1 Appeal while eligible
- first company Bestseller also +1 Reputation

## 22. Market Leader

Highest unit sales in the same genre/territory for two consecutive quarters earns Market Leader.

Effect: +1 Appeal in that territory while retained.

Lose when another product outsells it in a subsequent quarter.

## 23. Awards

Quality 4–5 products may enter eligible visible award opportunities.

Competition score uses Quality + Reputation + Campaign Investment + Sales Achievement.

Highest score wins; ties: Quality then sales.

Award effect:
- +1 permanent product Appeal
- +1 Company Reputation

No die roll.

## 24. Forecast Philosophy

Business conditions are not primarily surprise punishments.

NOW = exact known condition.
NEXT = exact known upcoming condition.
LATER SIGNAL = partial category information.

Players can plan around freight pressure, consumer booms, platform changes and other conditions.

## 25. Annual Accounts

At end Q4:
1. Finish annual sales/profit movements.
2. Pay staff/service annual costs due.
3. Pay base overhead.
4. Pay storage/excess inventory charges.
5. Pay loan interest.
6. Apply other annual expenses.
7. Calculate Pre-Tax Profit.
8. Apply simplified game corporation tax.
9. Record After-Tax Annual Profit.
10. Check $2M victory.
11. Reset current-year Profit to $0 for next year after recording result.
12. Advance Year and refresh annual Market Outlook.

Working tax:
- positive pre-tax profit up to $250K: 20%
- positive pre-tax profit above $250K: 25%
- loss: no corporation tax

Tax is a gameplay abstraction, not tax advice.

## 26. Annual Overhead

Working base overhead:
- Startup: $40K/year
- revenue over $1M: $70K/year
- revenue over $3M: $120K/year

Exact thresholds remain balance-test values.

## 27. International Expansion

Working setup costs:
- UK: starting territory
- EU: $60K
- USA: $100K

Products requiring localisation pay product/language costs.

Stock generally must physically be in the territory to sell there.

## 28. Player Count Scaling

### 2 Players
Use approximately 80% standard genre demand.

### 3 Players
Use standard demand.

### 4 Players
Use approximately 115% demand while maintaining tight factory/licence/retail competition.

Exact printed demand tracks will replace percentage calculations after balance testing.

## 29. Beginner Game

For a first learning game:
- UK only during Year 1
- Amazon and Website only
- China and Europe factories only
- no advanced company powers
- reduced card sets
- ignore Saturation in first year
- ignore ageing until Year 2

## 30. Guided First Turn

Example player chooses Dragon Realm from the Concept Market.

1. Secretly select DESIGN.
2. Reveal simultaneously with competitors.
3. Claim Dragon Realm when action resolves.
4. Pay development cost.
5. Place it in a Company Board product slot.
6. Set Base Appeal and Quality.
7. Decide future intended format/price/production route.
8. Observe competitors before choosing the next action.

The key is that selecting the product begins a chain of future decisions; it does not automatically guarantee demand.

## 31. Worked Physical Sale

A Standard Board Game sells 1K on UK Amazon at Standard price.

Printed channel contribution: $29K.
Batch Cost of Sales: $18K.

Move Cash +$29K.
Move Profit +$11K.
Remove one 1K Stock Cube.
Reduce available market demand by 1K.

No calculator is required.

## 33. Manufacturing Print Specification Requirement

A full production specification is mandatory before manufacturer quotation/final artwork.

It will cover every component including:
- final box external/internal dimensions
- box construction/material/thickness
- insert/tray design
- finished packed weight target
- board dimensions, folds, greyboard thickness and wrap
- Company Board material/thickness
- all card sizes, counts, stock/GSM, finish, corner radius and collation
- punchboard sheets, thickness, token dimensions and die lines
- wooden/plastic component dimensions, quantities and tolerances
- stock cubes and player colours
- rulebook/player aid size, pagination, paper and binding
- print colour specification
- bleed/safe areas
- varnish/lamination requirements
- barcode/ISBN/lot/batch/country-of-origin areas where applicable
- shrink-wrap/seals
- carton pack quantity
- master carton dimensions/weight targets
- palletisation assumptions
- compliance/testing requirements for intended markets
- pre-production proof requirements
- golden sample approval
- production tolerances
- spare/replacement component allowance
- QC/AQL expectations
- packaging drop/transit testing
- freight cube and fulfilment/storage optimisation

The current preliminary retail box target remains approximately **300 x 300 x 70 mm**, with a preferred packed mass below **2.3 kg**, but these are not locked until the component engineering is complete.

## 34. Decision-First Test

Whenever a rule is reviewed, ask:

**Does this create a meaningful decision, or merely administration/luck?**

If a player loses, they should usually be able to point to decisions such as price, quantity, timing, market choice, cost structure, cash management or competitor anticipation.
