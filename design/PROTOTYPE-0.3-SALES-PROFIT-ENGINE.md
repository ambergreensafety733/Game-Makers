# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 0.3 — Sales & Profit Engine

Goal: make sales-channel decisions strategically meaningful without turning the game into an accounting exercise.

**Design rule:** cards/player aids show pre-calculated contribution values. Players should rarely calculate percentages.

## 1. Sales Channels

The initial game uses five routes:

1. Amazon
2. Own Website
3. Etsy
4. Retail / Wholesale
5. Licensing

KDP is a separate print-on-demand route for books.

## 2. Channel Character

| Channel | Demand | Margin | Inventory Risk | Strategic Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Very High | Medium | Yes | Volume / international reach |
| Website | Medium initially | High | Yes | Best direct margin; improves with brand |
| Etsy | Low–Medium | High | Yes | Niche, books, compact/creative products |
| Retail/Wholesale | Medium–High | Low | Yes, but bulk movement | Large orders / stock clearance / reach |
| Licensing | Variable | Royalty | No publisher inventory | Low-risk international expansion |
| KDP | Medium | Fixed return | No | Low-risk book publishing |

## 3. Simplified UK VAT Principle

Physical games sold to UK consumers use pre-calculated UK net contribution values reflecting that the consumer price includes UK VAT and that selling channels charge fees/fulfilment.

Books are treated differently in the UK rules. Players do not perform VAT calculations during ordinary sales.

USA sales do not apply UK VAT. US tables therefore produce stronger gross revenue at an equivalent consumer price, offset by market-entry, fulfilment and freight considerations.

These are gameplay abstractions, not tax/accounting advice.

## 6. KDP Books

KDP does not use stock cubes.

Prototype value:

**1,000 KDP sales = $9K publisher income.**

No manufacturing order, freight, storage or stockout penalty.

KDP can therefore provide dependable low-risk income but cannot normally match the margin of a well-run physical direct-sales operation.

## 7. Direct Physical Books

Prototype direct book economics preserve the owner's real-world experience:

- Buy 1,000 books: $3K prototype stock cost.
- Website/Etsy sale contribution: approximately $13K per 1,000 sales at Standard pricing before general overhead/marketing.
- Approximate post-stock gross contribution: $10K per 1,000 sales.

Physical books use Small storage spaces and can run out of stock.

## 8. Retail / Wholesale

Retail is deliberately simple. Retailers buy stock in bulk at a large discount from RRP.

Prototype contribution received per 1,000 copies sold to retail:

| Format | Contribution Received | Demand Character |
|---|---:|---|
| Card | $11K | Easy bulk movement |
| Compact | $15K | Strong |
| Standard | $21K | Strong |
| Deluxe | $28K | Selective |
| Physical Book | $9K | Moderate |

The player still pays manufacturing/freight. Retail's attraction is that a contract may purchase 3K–10K units in one action and can clear warehouses quickly.

Retail Contract cards will specify exact quantities and territories.

## 9. Website Development

Website is not automatically a high-volume channel.

Website Level:
- Level 0: Closed
- Level 1: Storefront — max 1 Direct Demand per product/quarter
- Level 2: Growing Community — max 2
- Level 3: Established Brand — max 4
- Level 4: Major Direct Channel — max 6

Upgrade costs:
- Open Level 1: $30K
- Level 2: $50K
- Level 3: $80K
- Level 4: $120K

Some marketing/staff abilities reduce these costs or increase demand.

## 10. Etsy Access

Open Etsy: **$20K** setup/action cost.

Normal Etsy demand cap: 2 per product/quarter. Certain Books/Card/Compact products can reach 3–4 through concept bonuses and marketing.

## 11. International Market Entry

Prototype entry costs:

- UK: starting market
- EU: $60K to establish distribution/compliance/market presence
- USA: $100K to establish distribution/market presence

Once opened, the market remains available.

Language-dependent products may additionally require localisation.

## 12. Translation / Localisation

Prototype standard cost per product/language:

- Book: $20K
- Card/Compact: $20K
- Standard: $30K
- Deluxe: $30K

Language-independent games may pay 50% where only rules/packaging require localisation.

Initial languages planned: English, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Japanese and Korean.

### Award Token

## 14. Staff & Services

Recurring annual overhead:

| Staff / Service | Annual Cost | Benefit |
|---|---:|---|
| Accountant | $30K | Reduces annual admin/tax friction; later tax benefit |
| Marketing Manager | $60K | Marketing actions cost $10K less |
| Operations Manager | $70K | Freight costs reduced 20% |

| Translation Partner | $40K | Localisation costs 25% less |

Prototype rule: hiring costs one Business Action; salary is paid at Annual Accounts.

## 15. General Company Overhead

Every company pays a base annual overhead representing routine administration, software, insurance, stationery and miscellaneous business services.

- Startup: $40K/year
- If annual revenue exceeds $1M: $70K/year
- If annual revenue exceeds $3M: $120K/year

This prevents rapid scale from being cost-free.

## 16. Loans

Standard Business Loan:
- Receive $250K immediately.
- Pay $30K interest at each Annual Accounts while outstanding.
- Repay principal using one Business Action or at Annual Accounts.
- Maximum normal borrowing: $1M.

If a player cannot meet required payments, Emergency Finance rules will later apply. Bankruptcy should be possible but rare and recoverable enough to keep the player involved.

## 17. Factory Volume Discounts

Manufacturing costs from Prototype 0.2 are the 1K baseline.

For one production order:
- 1K–4K: normal cost
- 5K–9K: total manufacturing cost −10%
- 10K+: total manufacturing cost −20%

This creates the core inventory gamble: large runs improve margin but consume cash and warehouse space.

## 18. Factory Capacity

At the start of each quarter reveal Factory Capacity:

- China: normally 30 Production Units
- Europe: normally 20
- USA: normally 20

1 Production Unit = capacity for 1,000 Small copies. Medium uses 2 capacity; Large uses 3.

Players resolve manufacturing in turn/action order. Capacity shortages therefore create player interaction.

## 19. Licensing

Licensing avoids manufacturing, freight, storage and sales-channel costs.

Typical Licence Offer structure:
- Advance: $50K–$200K
- Royalty income: $3K–$12K per 1,000 licence sales
- Territory: specific market/language
- Duration: 2–4 years
- Minimum expected annual sales stated on card

The licensee controls stock. The player's game still gains some Reputation from strong licensed sales.

## 20. Simplified Annual Accounts

At year end use the Company Board player aid:

### A. Add
- Amazon contribution
- Website contribution
- Etsy contribution
- Retail contribution
- KDP income
- Licence advances/royalties
- Other income

### B. Subtract
- Development costs not already paid (normally costs are paid when actions occur)
- Manufacturing paid during year
- Freight
- Storage penalties
- Marketing
- Staff/services
- Base overhead
- Loan interest
- Event penalties

This gives **Pre-Tax Profit**.

### C. Simplified Corporation Tax
For gameplay only:
- Pre-tax profit up to $250K: pay 20%
- Pre-tax profit above $250K: pay 25%

Losses pay no corporation tax. A later advanced rule may allow loss carry-forward.

### D. Annual After-Tax Profit
This is the number compared with the **$2,000,000 victory target**.

## 21. Worked Decision Example

A player has a Standard Fantasy game at Standard price and expects 6K UK Amazon demand at Christmas.

They have no stock.

Options include:

**China 10K run:**
- Manufacturing baseline 10 × $14K = $140K
- 20% volume discount = $112K
- Medium freight: 2 units × $2K × 10 = $40K
- Total stock/freight investment = $152K
- Long lead time and substantial warehouse exposure

**Europe 6K run:**
- Manufacturing 6 × $19K = $114K
- 10% discount ≈ $103K
- Freight: 2 × $1K × 6 = $12K
- Total ≈ $115K
- Faster arrival, less excess inventory, but higher unit manufacturing cost

If UK Amazon Standard contribution is $29K per 1K sales, selling 6K produces $174K channel contribution before stock/freight and company overhead.

China may produce better long-run unit economics if the extra stock later sells. Europe can be safer if demand is uncertain. This is the intended style of decision throughout GAME MAKERS.

## 22. Important Balance Note

The current contribution/manufacturing numbers are intentionally provisional. They create the first coherent playable model but must be simulation- and table-tested. In particular we must test:

- Whether Amazon is too strong or too weak.
- Whether Website investment repays too quickly.
- Whether USA expansion dominates UK/EU.
- Whether large China print runs are too safe.
- Whether books can form a viable strategy without becoming dominant.
- Whether licensing is competitive with self-publishing.
- Whether the $2M target is reachable around Years 4–6.
