# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 0.7 — First Worked Four-Player Year 1

Purpose: test whether the current system produces understandable business decisions, whether the arithmetic is manageable, and whether the Year-1 economy is too generous or too harsh.

This is a **worked design test**, not final rules.

---

All companies begin with:
- Cash: $500K

Chosen Concept: **Dungeon Journal**

## Player D — Red Dice Publishing

Chosen Concept: **Family Frenzy**
Format: Compact Game

# 4. Q2 — Manufacturing and Channel Investment

Forecast now visible:
- Current: Direct Shopping Trend
- Next: Freight pressure
- Q4 Christmas known

## Dragon Forge — chooses PRODUCE

The Standard game completes its 2-quarter development path during Q2.

Dragon Forge places an **8K China order**.

Manufacturing:
- 8 × $14K = $112K
- 5K–9K volume discount 10% ≈ $101K

Freight estimate paid/reserved:
- Medium = 2 Freight Units
- China = $2K per unit per 1K
- 8 × 2 × $2K = $32K

Total production/freight commitment: **$133K**.

Cash after commitment: **$227K**.

Why 8K rather than 10K?
The player wants the China discount but does not want to tie up enough cash for a 10K+ run. This is a clear risk/reward choice.

Shipment enters **2Q pipeline**.

## Tiny Giant — chooses PRODUCE

Orders **10K Compact Games in China**.

Manufacturing:
- 10 × $8K = $80K
- 10K+ discount 20% = $64K

Freight:
- Small = 1 Freight Unit
- 10 × $2K = $20K

Total = **$84K**.

Cash: $430K − $84K = **$346K**.

The player takes a bigger inventory gamble to obtain the 20% production discount.

Shipment enters 2Q pipeline.

## Storyforge — chooses EXPAND

Opens Website Level 1 for $30K.

Cash = **$430K**.

The player delays physical book inventory because KDP remains available without stock.

## Red Dice — chooses PRODUCE

Orders **8K Compact Games in Europe**.

Manufacturing:
- 8 × $11K = $88K
- 10% volume discount ≈ $79K

Freight:
- 8 × $1K = $8K

Total ≈ **$87K**.

Cash = **$333K**.

The important decision:
Red Dice pays more per copy than Tiny Giant but expects to receive stock sooner and avoid a Christmas timing problem.

Shipment enters **1Q pipeline**.

# 8. Critical Finding — Year 1 Is Currently Too Punishing

All four plausible strategies produce accounting losses under the present contribution tables if all development and unsold production are charged immediately against annual profit.

This is useful prototype information.

The game has two possible fixes:

## Option A — Treat Unsold Inventory as an Asset for Profit Calculation

Manufacturing cost is assigned to copies actually sold; unsold copies stay as Inventory Value rather than being charged entirely to annual profit.

This is closer to real business accounting, but could add complexity.

## Option B — Use a Simplified 'Cost of Sales' Token System

Recommended.

Each Stock Cube carries a **Unit Cost marker** based on factory/source.

When a cube sells, subtract its pre-calculated Cost of Sales from channel contribution.

Unsold cubes remain in inventory and do not reduce annual profit until sold, although they still tie up cash and incur storage risk.

This gives the correct strategic distinction:
- cash can be low because money is tied up in inventory
- annual profit can still be positive
- excess inventory remains dangerous

This should replace the current practice of subtracting the full production run from annual profit immediately.

---

# 9. Recommended Simplified Profit Rule After This Test

For each 1K Stock Cube sold:

**Sales Contribution − Cube Cost of Sales = Product Gross Profit**

Then at Annual Accounts subtract:
- development expense
- marketing expense
- staff/services
- base overhead
- storage penalties
- interest
- other current-year expenses
- simplified corporation tax

Unsold inventory:
- reduces cash when purchased
- occupies warehouse space
- can become obsolete/decline
- is not charged again as profit expense until sold

This is both more realistic and strategically clearer.

# 10. Example Revised Dragon Forge Economics

Dragon Forge made 8K in China for manufacturing/freight total ≈ $133K.

Rounded Cost of Sales per 1K cube ≈ **$17K**.

Dragon Forge sells 5K at UK Amazon Standard:
- contribution = 5 × $29K = $145K
- Cost of Sales = 5 × $17K = $85K
- Product Gross Profit = **$60K**

Then subtract:
- $140K product development/art investment
- $20K marketing
- $40K overhead

Year 1 remains a loss because Dragon Forge made a deliberately expensive first product.

But the remaining 3K stock retains inventory value and can earn profit in Year 2.

A lower-development Compact or Book strategy should be able to approach break-even or modest Year-1 profit.

This creates differentiated company growth curves rather than forcing every strategy to earn immediate profit.

# 11. Decision Quality Observed in the Worked Year

The worked year produced several clear player-caused consequences:

### Dragon Forge
Decision: invest heavily in Quality and use slower China production.
Consequence: strong Christmas product and remaining stock, but weak Year-1 profitability.

### Tiny Giant
Decision: take the 10K China volume discount and invest in Website.
Consequence: good unit economics and future channel strength, but 3K unsold inventory.

### Storyforge
Decision: combine KDP with cheap physical books.
Consequence: safest cash position, but Website Level 1 limits the higher-margin physical strategy.

### Red Dice
Decision: pay more to manufacture in Europe.
Consequence: generates revenue one quarter earlier and finishes with strong cash, but loses Christmas sales by running out.

None of these outcomes required a lucky card or dice result.

# 12. Complexity Finding

The system is strategically interesting, but the following should be simplified on physical components:

1. Product Appeal should be calculated with a slider/track rather than repeated arithmetic.
2. Each product should have a Cost of Sales token so players do not recalculate manufacturing cost each sale.
3. Channel contribution should be printed on player-aid tables/cards.
4. Market demand should use cubes/markers rather than written numbers.
5. Inventory should remain physical Stock Cubes.
6. Forecast cards should be face-up in a three-card row.

The player should mostly move pieces and markers, not write calculations.

---

# 13. Year-1 Target Adjustment

Previous target: Year 1 profit $0–$150K.

After this worked test, recommended target becomes:
- Conservative/expensive first product: loss of $50K–$150K can be acceptable.
- Efficient Compact/Card/Book strategy: approximately break-even to +$75K.
- Exceptional first-year execution: up to approximately +$150K.

The important protection is that no reasonable opening strategy should leave a player unable to continue.
