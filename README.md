# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race

**Status:** Prototype 0.1 — active development

A competitive economic strategy board game for 2–4 players about building an independent games publishing company into an international success.

## Core Aim

Each player runs a games publisher. Design games and game books, manufacture products, manage inventory and cash flow, choose prices, market products, expand internationally, use Amazon/Etsy/direct website/retail/licensing channels, and control overheads.

**Primary victory target:** Be the first company to achieve **$2,000,000 annual after-tax profit**.

The design principle is: **easy arithmetic, difficult decisions**.

## Core Business Decisions

1. What should I make?
2. Where should I make it?
3. How many should I make?
4. What should I charge?
5. Where and how should I sell it?
6. When should I expand?

## Manufacturing and Inventory

Physical games may be manufactured in China, Europe or the USA. China generally has lower unit production cost but longer lead times and higher freight. European and US manufacture cost more but can replenish markets faster.

Physical stock uses a simplified size system:

- Small — 1 storage/freight unit
- Medium — 2 units
- Large — 3 units

Manufacturing orders move through a visible pipeline: **2 quarters away → 1 quarter away → warehouse**.

Ordering too much creates cash-flow and storage risk. Ordering too little risks stockouts and lost sales.

## Books — Two Business Models

Books deliberately have two selling strategies.

### KDP Print-on-Demand

Real-world design reference supplied by the project owner:

- RRP equivalent approximately £16.99
- Publisher receives approximately £7 per sale
- No publisher inventory
- No publisher freight or storage risk

### Buy Physical Books and Sell Direct

Real-world design reference supplied by the project owner:

- Publisher can buy books for approximately £2 each
- Selling through own website/Etsy can produce approximately £10 gross profit per sale
- Requires inventory, storage and fulfilment

This creates an intentional strategic choice: lower-risk KDP income versus higher-margin physical direct sales with inventory risk.

## Tax and VAT

Tax will influence decisions but will be deliberately simplified for gameplay. UK games and books will be treated differently where appropriate, and US sales will not use UK VAT. Player aids/cards should perform most calculations so players are not doing accounting exercises.

## Business Year

Each year contains:

1. Q1 — New Year
2. Q2 — Spring
3. Q3 — Autumn Build-Up
4. Q4 — Christmas Demand Boost
5. Annual Accounts & Tax

Each quarter is intended to follow a simple **Plan → Act → Market → Sell** rhythm, with approximately three Business Actions per player.

## Company Dashboard

Each player board tracks:

- Cash
- Annual profit
- Reputation
- Company level
- Product portfolio
- UK/EU/USA inventory
- Sales channels
- Staff/services
- Overheads
- Loans/cash flow

## Reputation

Companies build a 0–10 reputation. Higher reputation improves launches, licensing opportunities and market performance.

## Product Life Cycle

Products can progress through:

**Launch → Growth → Mature → Decline**

Advertising, expansions, translations and new editions can extend or revive products.

## Demand

Working simplified formula:

**Demand = Base Demand + Market Trend + Marketing + Reputation − Price Penalty**

The market should be visible enough that players can plan, while competitors' decisions can change the outcome.

## Competition

Players compete indirectly and directly for:

- Customer demand
- Factory capacity
- Retail contracts
- Advertising opportunities
- Licensing deals
- Awards/conventions
- International market position

The game should avoid destructive take-that mechanics as its main interaction. Competition should arise primarily from business decisions.

- Reputation 1
- Amazon UK initially available
- No staff
- No published products
- Starter concept plus a random Game Concept

## Expected Profit Curve for Balancing

Early balancing target only:

- Year 1: $0–$150K
- Year 2: $100K–$400K
- Year 3: $300K–$800K
- Year 4: $600K–$1.3M
- Year 5: $1M–$2M+

The target experience is normally to produce a winner around Year 5, with excellent/aggressive play potentially winning earlier.

## Visual Direction

The game should be colourful, captivating and immediately readable rather than resembling a spreadsheet. Markets, factories, products, warehouses and shipping should be physically visible on the table. Illustrated products, strong icons, colourful territories, stock cubes, Bestseller/Hit Game tokens and company dashboards should make the growth of each publisher visually satisfying.
