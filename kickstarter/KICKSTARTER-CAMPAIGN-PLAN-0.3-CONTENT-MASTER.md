# 4. FAST GAME SUMMARY

Use a graphic strip:
**2–4 PLAYERS | STRATEGIC ECONOMIC GAME | 120–180 MIN TARGET | AGES 14+ WORKING TARGET**

Working age/time are subject to blind playtest confirmation.

Campaign copy:
GAME MAKERS is an economic strategy game inspired by the real decisions independent publishers face — simplified into a competitive tabletop experience rather than a business spreadsheet.

There are no random sales rolls. Market information, competitors, lead times and opportunities create uncertainty, but players choose how to respond.

# 5. THE SIX DECISIONS

Create six highly visual panels.

## DESIGN
Choose concepts, formats and Quality. A cheap card game reaches market differently from a premium deluxe strategy game.

## PRODUCE
China may deliver the best unit cost — if you can predict demand months ahead. Faster factories cost more.

## MARKET
Spend money to build demand, but only where the additional sales justify it.

## EXPAND
Build direct sales and enter new territories. Growth creates opportunity — and overhead.

## DEALS
Take retailer orders and licence successful products internationally, or keep control and chase the margin yourself.

## FINANCE
Protect Cash. Stock sitting in a warehouse cannot pay your bills.

Visual required: the six Action tiles around a central company logo.

# 6. HOW A QUARTER WORKS — UNDER 90 SECONDS

Graphic flow:
**FORECAST -> CHOOSE -> REVEAL -> ALLOCATE -> PRICE -> SELL -> ACCOUNT -> SHIP**

Copy:
At the start of each quarter you can see today's market and information about what may be coming. Every player then secretly chooses business actions.

Will you launch another product while a rival builds marketing? Order cheap stock from China while another pays more for speed? Push Premium pricing while somebody else attacks with Budget?

Then allocate your inventory, reveal prices and compete for actual market demand.

The strongest offer sells first — until the customers or stock run out.

---

# 7. THE SIGNATURE DECISION — INVENTORY

Headline:
**10,000 CHEAP COPIES AREN'T CHEAP IF NOBODY BUYS THEM.**

Explain with one illustrated example:
Player A orders 10K from China at a low unit cost but waits two quarters.
Player B orders 5K from Europe at higher cost and reaches a hot market first.

Show possible outcomes:
- A earns more margin if demand remains.
- B captures customers before A arrives.
- A risks storage/excess inventory.
- B risks stockout if demand explodes.

This should become one of the campaign's strongest gameplay demonstrations.

---

# 8. PRICING BATTLE

Headline:
**HOW MUCH IS YOUR HIT WORTH?**

Budget: easier to sell, lower margin.
Standard: balanced.
Premium: harder to sell, higher return when brand/Quality support it.

Show three face-down price markers, then reveal simultaneously.

Campaign message: You do not know your rivals' price when you commit yours.

---

# 9. CHANNEL STRATEGY

Headline:
**REACH OR MARGIN?**

Show four routes:
- Marketplace/Amazon: reach and volume, lower contribution
- Website/direct: better margin, must build capacity/audience
- KDP game books: no physical inventory, lower risk
- Licensing: advance/royalty without manufacturing that territory yourself

Add physical books/direct channel as the higher-margin inventory alternative to KDP.

---

# 10. GLOBAL EXPANSION

Visual map: UK -> EU -> USA -> licence partners/world.

Copy:
Opening another territory gives access to more customers — but adds management cost, inventory decisions and logistics. Sometimes the smartest global move is not opening a warehouse at all: licence the product to somebody else.

# 23. RISKS & CHALLENGES

Use specific rather than boilerplate wording.

Cover:
- final manufacturing tolerances
- freight/port delays
- customs/tax changes
- component sourcing
- print/colour corrections
- regional fulfilment delays
- unexpectedly high demand

Then state mitigation:
- multiple supplier quotations
- conservative delivery schedule
- production/QC checkpoints
- financial contingency
- regional fulfilment planning
- replacement stock reserve
- regular transparent backer updates

Never claim a Kickstarter has 'no risk'.
