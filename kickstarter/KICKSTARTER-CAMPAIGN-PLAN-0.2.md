# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Kickstarter Campaign Plan 0.2 — Scale Economics, Backer Mix & Launch Gates

This model extends Plan 0.1. All figures are planning assumptions until replaced by supplier, freight, fulfilment, advertising and tax quotations.

# 1. Working reward mix
Model an eventual campaign mix rather than assuming every backer buys the £55 core game.

Planning mix:
- 70% Core at £55
- 20% Founder's Edition at £75
- 8% two-copy Business Partners at £105
- 2% other/supporter/retailer equivalent

This gives a working average collected product pledge of approximately **£62–£64 per backer**, excluding separately collected shipping/tax. Use **£63** for planning.

# 2. Production volume assumptions
Illustrative ex-factory core-game cost curve:
- 1,000 copies: £15.50 each
- 2,500: £12.75
- 5,000: £10.75
- 10,000: £9.25

These are targets/assumptions, NOT quotations.

Do not manufacture exactly the number of Kickstarter orders. Allow copies for replacements, reviewers, future direct sales and retail, but avoid speculative inventory.

Working production quantities:
- ~1,000 backers: manufacture 1,250–1,500
- ~2,500 backers: 3,000
- ~5,000 backers: 6,000
- ~10,000 backers: 11,500–12,000

# 3. Gross campaign scenarios at £63 average pledge
Excluding shipping/tax:
- 500 backers: £31,500
- 1,000: £63,000
- 2,500: £157,500
- 5,000: £315,000
- 10,000: £630,000

Conservative Kickstarter/payment reserve at 9%:
- 500: £2,835
- 1,000: £5,670
- 2,500: £14,175
- 5,000: £28,350
- 10,000: £56,700

Net campaign receipts before production/marketing/fixed costs:
- 500: £28,665
- 1,000: £57,330
- 2,500: £143,325
- 5,000: £286,650
- 10,000: £573,300

# 4. Manufacturing cash requirement examples
Using working production quantities/cost assumptions:
- 1,500 copies x £15.50 = £23,250
- 3,000 copies x ~£12.50 = £37,500
- 6,000 copies x ~£10.50 = £63,000
- 12,000 copies x ~£9.00 = £108,000

Actual quotations will likely include tooling/proofs separately or embedded differently.

# 5. Freight/inbound reserve
Until quotes exist, reserve roughly **£5 per manufactured copy** for international freight/import/inbound handling planning.

Illustrative:
- 1,500 = £7,500
- 3,000 = £15,000
- 6,000 = £30,000
- 12,000 = £60,000

This is intentionally conservative and must be replaced with container/carton/region quotes.

# 6. Fixed campaign reserve
Retain Plan 0.1 working fixed reserve of £30,000 covering artwork, prototypes, campaign assets, reviewer copies, marketing tests, prepress/tooling allowance, professional review and contingency.

Some of this may be spent before Kickstarter and therefore represents publisher investment rather than campaign cash requirement; track both views separately.

# 7. Paid marketing / CAC
CAC = paid advertising cost divided by paid-attributed backers.

Initial planning rules:
- excellent CAC: £8–£12
- acceptable test range: £12–£18
- warning range: £18–£22
- normally stop/adjust campaigns above £22 unless that audience has materially higher pledge value

At a £63 average pledge, advertising must not be scaled merely because ROAS looks positive; calculate contribution after Kickstarter fees, product cost and fulfilment reserves.

Pre-launch ads should be judged by qualified lead/follower cost and eventual conversion, not cheap clicks.

# 8. Organic versus paid target
Aim for at least **60% of backers from organic/community/Kickstarter/reviewer/email sources** and no more than about 40% requiring directly paid acquisition in the base model.

The stronger the organic share, the safer the campaign economics.

# 9. Day-1 launch target
A campaign should look successful quickly.

If final public funding goal is £50K, preferred internal target is to enter launch with enough committed audience to produce **£20K–£30K in the first 24 hours** and a credible path to funding in 48–72 hours.

At £63 average pledge:
- £20K ≈ 318 backers
- £25K ≈ 397
- £30K ≈ 476

Do not launch merely because a pre-launch page has many followers. Measure purchase intent and historical conversion from the actual audience sources.

# 10. Pre-launch audience target
Before final launch approval, seek an audience capable of delivering roughly 400 first-day backers.

Illustrative conversion planning:
- 35% conversion requires ~1,143 highly qualified prospects
- 25% requires ~1,600
- 20% requires ~2,000

Therefore an initial practical target is **1,500–2,500 high-intent email/pre-launch prospects**, with evidence that a substantial portion actually wants this game rather than merely entered a giveaway.

# 11. Founder Edition economics
Working Founder's uplift: £20 above core.

Target incremental manufacturing/packing cost: **no more than £6–£8**.

That preserves meaningful contribution while giving backers visible value.

Avoid Founder upgrades that:
- require a second completely different box size
- create a separate heavy insert
- add £10+ freight-weight consequences
- require unique gameplay balancing

Best candidates are premium markers, cosmetic treatments, extra company identities and a Founder card/certificate.

# 12. Stretch-goal financial gate
Before announcing any stretch goal calculate:
**incremental unit cost x every qualifying copy + incremental freight + tooling + fulfilment complexity + replacement reserve.**

Example: a £1.20 upgrade across 5,000 copies costs £6,000 before freight/tooling. A £5,000 increase in campaign funding would therefore NOT safely pay for it.

Stretch thresholds must be based on incremental contribution, not gross funding.

# 13. Regional fulfilment planning
Model at least:
- UK
- European Union
- USA
- Canada
- Australia/New Zealand
- Rest of World / unsupported destinations

For each region obtain:
- inbound freight to hub
- pick/pack fee
- packaging
- postage by packed weight
- storage
- returns/replacements
- tax/customs handling

Do not publish final shipping prices until these are sufficiently firm.

# 14. VAT/tax cash-flow protection
Tax collected on behalf of authorities is not campaign profit.

The financial workbook must keep separate columns for:
- product pledge revenue
- shipping collected
- VAT/sales tax collected
- Kickstarter/payment fees
- manufacturing
- freight/import
- fulfilment/postage
- marketing
- fixed development/campaign cost
- contingency
- corporation tax/profit after delivery

# 15. Cash timing
Kickstarter success does not mean all cash is immediately free to spend.

Build a cash calendar with:
1. pre-launch artwork/prototypes/ads
2. Kickstarter receipts after successful collection
3. factory deposit
4. final manufacturing payment
5. freight/import payments
6. fulfilment deposits
7. pledge-manager shipping/tax receipts
8. final postage/fulfilment
9. replacement reserve retained after delivery

Never spend the delivery reserve on post-campaign retail inventory.

# 16. 500-backers scenario
Treat 500 as an important failure/defence scenario.

At £63 average pledge gross = £31.5K. This is below the provisional £45–50K goal and should therefore not fund under the current plan. That is desirable if safe manufacture/delivery cannot be supported.

Do not lower the goal merely to convert a 500-backer campaign into a funded campaign.

# 17. 1,000-backers scenario
Gross ~£63K.

This can potentially manufacture the project but margins remain tight once fixed launch investment, freight and spare inventory are included. It should be considered a successful launch/community result but not assumed to be highly profitable.

The campaign must be designed to deliver safely at this level.

# 18. 2,500-backers scenario
Gross ~£157.5K.

Manufacturing efficiency improves substantially. This is the first scenario likely to produce attractive commercial economics while also leaving sensible future-sale inventory, assuming marketing and fulfilment are controlled.

This should be the **base success ambition** for campaign planning.

# 19. 5,000-backers scenario
Gross ~£315K.

This becomes a substantial international launch. Before going live, suppliers and fulfilment partners must confirm they can scale to this volume without changing promised dates/quality.

At this level, stretch-goal temptation becomes dangerous: protect margin and delivery simplicity.

# 20. 10,000-backers scenario
Gross ~£630K.

Treat this as an over-success contingency, not the forecast.

Before launch have a written response plan covering:
- manufacturing capacity
- additional QC
- split freight/container planning
- regional hub capacity
- customer service
- replacement stock
- cash/tax controls

A £630K Kickstarter is a fulfilment operation, not merely a larger version of a £50K campaign.

# 21. Campaign traffic funnel
Build measurement around:
Awareness -> campaign/pre-launch visit -> follower/email lead -> launch click -> pledge -> pledge retained -> successfully collected payment -> delivered backer.

Track conversion at every stage. The real KPI is delivered profitable backers, not impressions or social followers.

# 22. Launch approval gates
Do not approve launch until all GREEN:
- GAME: repeated blind playtests validate rules and 2/3/4-player balance
- PRODUCT: final component list/box dimensions/weight substantially locked
- COST: at least two comparable manufacturing quotes
- LOGISTICS: fulfilment quotes for UK/EU/USA and credible ROW policy
- TAX: VAT/sales-tax/import structure reviewed professionally
- AUDIENCE: enough qualified prospects for target first 24–48 hours
- CREATIVE: campaign page, trailer, photography/renders, reviews/previews ready
- CASH: low-success and over-success cash-flow scenarios both survivable
- DEFENCE: supplier backup, contingency and delay communication plan ready
