## WHAT WILL YOUR COMPANY BECOME?

Will you build premium strategy games with high margins?

Flood the market with inexpensive card games?

Build a direct-sales machine?

Publish game books without risking warehouses full of stock?

Licence your hits around the world?

Or combine all of them into a company your rivals never anticipated?

There is no prescribed route to $2 million. You build the business — and live with the consequences.

---

## EVERY QUARTER IS A BUSINESS DECISION

You can see the market. You have clues about what may be coming. You can see your rivals' companies.

What you cannot see is exactly what they are about to do.

Every quarter, players secretly choose business Actions, then reveal them together.

**DESIGN** — create and improve products.

**PRODUCE** — manufacture inventory and choose where it comes from.

**MARKET** — spend to increase demand.

**EXPAND** — develop direct sales and international markets.

**DEALS** — pursue retailers and international licences.

**FINANCE** — protect liquidity and fund growth.

Then comes the decision everybody has been waiting for: **how much will you charge?**

---

## CHEAP. FAST. OR BOTH? YOU PROBABLY CAN'T HAVE ALL THREE.

Your new game is ready.

A Chinese factory can manufacture a large run at an excellent unit cost — but it will take longer to reach you.

A European factory can get stock to market sooner — but your margin is smaller.

US manufacturing can solve a domestic shortage quickly — at a price.

Order too little and your bestseller sells out while competitors take the customers.

Order too much and your Cash becomes boxes sitting in a warehouse.

**Inventory is opportunity. Inventory is also risk.**

---

## 10,000 CHEAP COPIES AREN'T CHEAP IF NOBODY BUYS THEM

Imagine two companies spot the same opportunity.

**Company A** orders 10,000 copies cheaply from China.

**Company B** pays more and makes 5,000 in Europe.

Company B reaches the market first.

If demand stays strong, Company A may eventually make the better margin.

If the market changes, Company A may have thousands of unwanted games to store.

If demand explodes, Company B may sell out and watch Company A take the remaining customers.

Neither player rolled for the answer. **They chose the risk.**

---

## HOW MUCH IS YOUR HIT WORTH?

Players secretly set product prices before the market resolves.

**BUDGET** — easier to sell, less profit per copy.

**STANDARD** — balanced price and demand.

**PREMIUM** — greater return if your Quality, reputation and market support it.

Then everyone reveals.

Did your rival undercut you?

Did they go Premium, leaving the mass market open?

Did you both chase the same customers?

The strongest offers sell first — until the customers or the stock run out.

---

## REACH OR MARGIN?

Selling the game is only half the decision.

**MARKETPLACES** can deliver enormous reach, but every sale carries costs.

**YOUR OWN WEBSITE** can produce better margins — once you build enough direct demand and capacity.

**KDP GAME BOOKS** require no physical inventory and can generate reliable contribution without a warehouse.

**PHYSICAL BOOKS** can make more per direct sale — but now you own the stock risk.

**LICENSING** lets another publisher manufacture and sell your game in another territory while you collect an advance and royalties.

The highest Revenue does not automatically win.

## PROFIT WINS.

---

## GROW — BUT DON'T BECOME TOO EXPENSIVE TO RUN

Success unlocks bigger companies, more actions and international opportunities.

It also creates overhead.

More products cost money to manage.

More territories require infrastructure.

Loans accelerate growth — and charge interest.

Warehouses full of slow stock cost money.

GAME MAKERS rewards growth when growth is profitable.

---

## YOU ARE COMPETING WITH THE PEOPLE AT THE TABLE

This is not four players independently solving spreadsheets.

You compete for:
- market demand
- factory capacity
- Concepts
- retailer opportunities
- licence deals
- genre leadership
- customers

If a competitor sells out, you may capture the customers they cannot serve.

If everybody chases Fantasy, another genre may suddenly become a very attractive place to build a business.

Interaction comes from **commercial competition**, not arbitrary 'destroy another player's company' cards.

Revenue is impressive.

Cash is essential.

But after manufacturing, overheads, storage, interest and tax, one number decides the race:

# ANNUAL AFTER-TAX PROFIT

The first company to reach **$2,000,000** wins.

## A GAME ABOUT MAKING GAMES — FROM PEOPLE WHO ACTUALLY MAKE THEM

GAME MAKERS began with real independent publishing decisions: creating games, sourcing manufacture, importing stock, selling through marketplaces and direct channels, publishing game books and licensing products into other languages and territories.

We took those decisions and asked a different question:

**Which of them would make a great strategy game?**

The result is not intended to be accountancy homework. It is a competitive race built around risk, timing, margins, growth and reading the other companies at the table.

# I. FAQ — WORKING ANSWERS

**How much luck is involved?**
Markets and opportunities can change, but the core sales system does not ask you to roll dice to see whether your product sells. The challenge is interpreting information and positioning your company better than your rivals.

**Is this a business simulation?**
It is inspired by real publishing decisions, but it is designed first as a strategy board game. Real-world processes are simplified whenever extra realism would create administration rather than interesting choices.

**Does it work with two players?**
Yes. The working two-player system uses Neutral Competitors to preserve pressure in markets without creating a complicated automated opponent.

**Will it come to retail?**
That is the intention after Kickstarter fulfilment. Kickstarter backers are helping make the first commercial production possible.

**Are Kickstarter exclusives essential gameplay?**
No. The Core Edition is intended to be a complete strategic game. Founder/Kickstarter upgrades should be premium, cosmetic or additional tested variety rather than missing essential rules.

**Why use dollars when the publisher is British?**
The game represents companies competing internationally and the $2 Million Race is a clear global objective. The currency is game money rather than a tax/accounting representation of any single country.

**Will other languages be available?**
To be confirmed after demand, localisation cost and production quantities are known. Do not promise a language until translation, proofing and minimum quantities are viable.

**What about VAT/customs/shipping?**
Final campaign wording will show region estimates and tax treatment after fulfilment partners and professional tax advice confirm the structure. Shipping will be charged separately rather than hidden in the product pledge.
