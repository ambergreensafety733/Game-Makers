# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Rulebook — Prototype 0.1

> This is the evolving rulebook. Mechanics and values will be updated during development and playtesting.

## 1. Aim of the Game

Players compete to build the most successful international games publishing company. The first company to achieve **$2,000,000 annual after-tax profit** wins, subject to final end-of-year confirmation.

Players make strategic decisions about product design, manufacturing location, production quantity, pricing, sales channels, inventory, marketing, languages, licensing, staffing and expansion.

The intended design philosophy is **easy arithmetic, difficult decisions**.

## 2. Players and Playing Time

- 2–4 players
- Target playing time: 90–150 minutes
- Target age: 12+

## 3. Starting Position

Prototype starting position for each player:

- Cash: $500,000
- Reputation: 1
- Amazon UK sales channel available
- No staff
- No published products
- One Starter Concept
- One random Game Concept

## 4. The Business Year

A business year consists of:

- Q1 — New Year
- Q2 — Spring
- Q3 — Autumn Build-Up
- Q4 — Christmas
- Annual Accounts & Tax

Q4 receives a Christmas demand advantage. Exact demand values will be established during balancing.

## 5. Quarter Structure

Working structure:

**PLAN → ACT → MARKET → SELL**

Each company receives approximately three Business Actions per quarter.

## 6. Business Actions

Working action list:

- Develop a Game
- Place Manufacturing Order
- Launch Product
- Advertise
- Open Market
- Hire Staff/Service
- Translate Product
- Negotiate Licence
- Change Price
- Expand Warehouse
- Take Loan

## 7. Products

Product formats currently include:

- Game Book
- Card Game
- Compact Game
- Standard Board Game
- Deluxe Board Game
- Expansion

Physical products have Small, Medium or Large size classifications affecting freight and storage.

## 8. Manufacturing

Players choose where to manufacture physical products. China generally offers the lowest unit production cost but longer delivery. Europe and the USA provide faster replenishment at higher unit cost.

Orders move through the production/shipping pipeline until they reach the relevant warehouse.

## 9. Inventory and Stockouts

Players must decide how much stock to hold. Too much stock ties up cash and may create storage charges. Too little stock can cause lost sales.

Repeated stockouts may reduce Company Reputation.

## 10. Books

### KDP Print-on-Demand

KDP books require no publisher inventory, freight or storage. Current real-world design reference is an RRP equivalent of approximately £16.99 with approximately £7 returned to the publisher per sale.

### Physical Direct-Sale Books

Players may instead buy physical books into inventory and sell them through their website/Etsy. Current real-world design reference is approximately £2 purchase cost and approximately £10 gross profit per direct sale.

Physical books therefore offer higher potential margin but introduce stock, storage and fulfilment risk.

## 11. Markets

Initial markets:

- UK
- Europe
- USA

Sales channels include Amazon, own website, Etsy, retail/wholesale and licensing.

## 12. Demand

Working demand calculation:

**Base Demand + Market Trend + Marketing + Reputation − Price Penalty**

The intention is that the board/cards make this calculation quick and visible.

## 13. Pricing

Products will generally offer simple pricing choices such as Budget, Standard and Premium. Lower prices increase demand but reduce margin. Higher prices increase margin but can reduce demand.

## 14. Marketing

Planned marketing choices include Amazon advertising, social media, conventions, influencers, awards campaigns and direct-community/email development.

## 15. Reputation

Company Reputation runs from 0–10 and influences launches, reviews, licensing and market performance.

## 16. Product Life Cycle

Products progress through:

**Launch → Growth → Mature → Decline**

Players may use advertising, expansions, translations and new editions to extend or revive products.

## 17. Licensing

Licence cards offer advances and/or royalties in return for allowing another publisher to exploit a game in a particular territory/language. Licensing avoids much of the manufacturing and inventory risk but normally gives lower profit per copy than successful self-publishing.

## 18. Staff and Overheads

Staff and external services provide advantages but create recurring overhead. Examples include accountancy, marketing, operations, sales, warehousing and translation.

## 19. VAT and Tax

VAT and corporation tax will affect the economics, but the board game will simplify them. Player aids and product/channel tables should show net results wherever possible rather than requiring repeated tax calculations.

## 20. Annual Accounts

At the end of Q4, each company completes Annual Accounts. The working calculation is:

**Revenue − Direct Costs − Freight/Fulfilment − Storage − Marketing − Staff/Overheads − Interest − Simplified Tax = Annual After-Tax Profit**

Exact procedure will be simplified and illustrated in later versions.

## 21. Winning

The first company to confirm **$2,000,000 annual after-tax profit** at Annual Accounts wins.

A maximum-year/tiebreak rule will be established after playtesting.

## 22. Advanced Companies

The final game is intended to include ten selectable companies. Beginner mode will use identical abilities; advanced mode will give each company a different strategic advantage.
