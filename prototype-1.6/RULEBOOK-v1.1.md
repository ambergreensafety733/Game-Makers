# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Rulebook v1.1 — Prototype 1.6

**2–4 players | Standard Game: up to 5 Business Years | Target: 120–180 minutes**

You run an independent games publisher. Design games and game books, decide where and how many to manufacture, manage cash and stock, set prices, advertise, expand into new territories, build direct sales, compete for retailers and licences, and race to become the first company making **$2 million annual after-tax profit**.

The game is deliberately decision-first. Core sales are not decided by dice.

---

# 1. HOW TO WIN

At Annual Accounts, calculate each company's annual after-tax Profit.

If one company has reached **$2,000K ($2M) after-tax Profit**, it wins.

If more than one crosses $2M in the same year, highest after-tax Profit wins; then Cash; then Reputation.

If nobody reaches $2M by the end of Year 5 in the Standard Game, highest Year-5 after-tax Profit wins.

Short Game: 3 years, highest final after-tax Profit wins unless somebody reaches $2M earlier.

Epic Game: up to 6 years.

---

# 2. THE IMPORTANT NUMBERS

Money is normally expressed in **$K**. $20K means $20,000.

One physical Stock Cube represents **1,000 copies**.

Cash and Profit are different:
- **Cash** = money available to spend.
- **Profit** = economic result for the current Business Year.
- **Revenue** = sales/deal income used to determine Company Scale.

Manufacturing stock reduces Cash immediately, but unsold inventory does not immediately reduce Profit. The Cost of Sales is recognised when stock sells.

---

# 3. STARTING A COMPANY

Each player receives:
- 1 universal Company Board
- 6 Action tiles
- player markers/cubes
- 1 Player Aid
- $500K starting Cash
- Profit $0K
- Revenue $0K
- Reputation 1
- Website Level 0
- Startup Scale
- Amazon UK access
- no products, staff, loans or foreign territories

Choose a Company identity. For a first game, ignore Company powers.

---

# 4. CENTRAL SETUP

1. Set Business Year to Year 1 and Quarter to Q1.
2. Reveal the Year 1 Market Outlook.
3. Set the correct demand tracks for the player count.
4. Prepare the Forecast row: NOW, NEXT, LATER SIGNAL.
5. Reveal 5 Concept cards.
6. Reveal 3 Licence Offers.
7. Reveal 3 Retail/Opportunity cards.
8. Display available Factory cards.
9. Choose a First Company randomly. The marker passes clockwise each quarter.

### Recommended First Game
Use only:
- UK
- Amazon and Website
- China and Europe factories
- reduced Concept/Forecast/Licence/Retail decks
- no Company powers
- no Saturation during Year 1
- no ageing until Year 2

---

# 5. BUSINESS YEAR

Each year contains:
- Q1
- Q2
- Q3
- Q4 CHRISTMAS
- ANNUAL ACCOUNTS

Each quarter follows:
1. Forecast
2. Market Update
3. Business Round A
4. Business Round B
5. Allocation & Price
6. Sales
7. Finance
8. Pipeline
9. Quarter End

---

# 6. FORECAST

The Forecast row gives players information before conditions affect them.

**NOW** — exact current effect.

**NEXT** — exact condition expected next quarter.

**LATER SIGNAL** — partial information such as FREIGHT PRESSURE, CONSUMER BOOM or PLATFORM CHANGE.

At the appropriate point, advance the cards and reveal exact information.

Forecasts are intended to create planning decisions rather than surprise punishment.

---

# 7. BUSINESS ROUNDS

There are two Business Rounds per quarter.

For each round:
1. Every player secretly selects one Action tile.
2. Place it face-down.
3. Reveal all simultaneously.
4. Resolve independent actions together where possible.
5. Use First Company/contest rules only where players want the same limited resource.

A player normally cannot use the same Action category twice in the same quarter.

The six Actions are:
- DESIGN
- PRODUCE
- MARKET
- EXPAND
- DEALS
- FINANCE

---

# 8. DESIGN

Use DESIGN to create or improve products.

### Take a Concept
Choose one of the five face-up Concept cards, pay its development cost and place it in an empty Product slot. Refill the market.

### Custom Concept
Instead of taking a card, pay the Custom Concept surcharge and create a Base Appeal 3 product in a chosen genre, subject to current rules.

### Quality
Products have Quality 1–5. Quality investment is deliberate and paid for; it is never rolled randomly.

Quality Appeal bonus:
- Quality 1: +0
- Quality 2: +1
- Quality 3: +2
- Quality 4: +3
- Quality 5: +4

Some Concepts/formats have additional development requirements.

### Contested Concept
If several DESIGN players want the same Concept, they may bid $0K/$10K/$20K/$30K secretly. Highest bid pays and takes it. Ties: lower current annual Profit, then First Company order.

---

# 9. PRODUCT FORMATS

Core formats:
- Card Game
- Compact Game
- Standard Board Game
- Deluxe Board Game
- Game Book

Larger formats generally cost more to develop, manufacture, ship and store, but can support higher selling prices.

Books may use KDP or physical direct stock depending on route.

---

# 10. PRODUCE

Use PRODUCE to order physical inventory.

Choose:
1. Product.
2. Factory/source.
3. Quantity in 1K units.
4. Destination/route where required.

Pay the full production/landed cost from Cash immediately.

Take or record the appropriate **Cost of Sales per cube** for that batch.

Place order/stock cubes in the Shipping Pipeline according to lead time.

### Prototype Cost of Sales per 1K

China 5K–9K:
- Card $7K
- Compact $10K
- Standard $18K
- Deluxe $30K

China 10K+:
- Card $6K
- Compact $9K
- Standard $16K
- Deluxe $27K

Europe 5K–9K:
- Card $8K
- Compact $12K
- Standard $22K
- Deluxe $37K

USA 5K–9K:
- Card $9K
- Compact $14K
- Standard $24K
- Deluxe $38K

Other run sizes use the current Player Aid/table.

### Factory Competition
Factories have visible capacity. If orders exceed capacity, players may reduce orders. Remaining conflict uses a sealed Priority Premium bid. Highest premium receives capacity first. Losing players may use another available factory or cancel without paying the manufacturing cost.

---

# 11. SHIPPING & STOCK

China normally has the longest lead time and lowest large-run cost.

Europe normally costs more but arrives faster.

USA domestic manufacturing is useful for US supply and rapid production but normally has higher unit costs.

When production arrives, move stock to the relevant Warehouse/Territory.

Physical products cannot normally be sold in a territory without stock there.

---

# 12. MARKET

Use MARKET to increase demand/Appeal.

Prototype examples:
- Amazon Ads: $20K, +2 for the chosen Amazon product this quarter.
- Micro Influencers: $10K, +1.
- Social Campaign: $20K, +1 broad effect.
- Established Creator: $30K, +2.
- Major Campaign: $60K, +4.
- Community Building: $30K, supports Website growth/demand according to the card/track.

Marketing has a known effect. It does not require a success roll.

Temporary marketing ends at Quarter End unless stated otherwise.

---

# 13. EXPAND

Use EXPAND for business infrastructure.

Typical choices:
- open/upgrade Website
- establish EU sales
- establish USA sales
- open an eligible additional channel
- localise a product/language
- increase warehouse capability

Working foreign territory setup:
- EU: $60K
- USA: $100K

International operations create annual management overhead, so expansion is not automatically profitable.

---

# 14. WEBSITE

Website sales normally give higher margins but lower capacity than Amazon until developed.

Capacity per product/quarter:
- Level 1: 1K
- Level 2: 2K
- Level 3: 4K
- Level 4: 6K

Capacity does not create customers. The product still needs sufficient Appeal and demand.

---

# 15. KDP & GAME BOOKS

Eligible game books can sell through KDP without physical inventory.

Prototype value per 1K KDP sales:
- Cash +$9K
- Profit +$9K

Physical books can instead be manufactured cheaply and sold through Website/Etsy/other direct channels for a higher potential gross profit, but require Cash, inventory and storage.

Working physical book landed cost: $3K per 1K.

This creates the intended decision: lower-risk KDP versus higher-margin direct inventory.

---

# 16. DEALS

Use DEALS to pursue visible Licence Offers, Retail Contracts and eligible Opportunities.

### Licensing
Licence cards specify:
- advance
- royalty
- expected/eligible sales
- territory
- term
- product requirements

Licence advances and royalties increase Cash and Profit when received.

Working limit: maximum three active external territory licences per product.

If several players pursue the same licence:
1. eligibility
2. Quality
3. Reputation
4. if tied, players secretly offer to accept a reduced advance; lowest acceptable advance wins

### Retail
Retail cards specify stock, Quality, price or other requirements. Retail can move significant stock quickly but usually gives lower margin than direct sales.

Contested retailers use the retailer's printed priority, then sealed discount offers if required.

---

# 17. FINANCE

Use FINANCE to manage liquidity.

Standard loan:
- receive $250K Cash
- annual interest $30K while outstanding

Use FINANCE to take/repay eligible loans or use another visible finance option.

Borrowing can fund growth but reduces annual Profit through interest.

---

# 18. ALLOCATION & PRICE

After Business Round B, players prepare stock for the Sales phase.

### Inventory Allocation
Allocate available stock to opened territories/channels. Allocation is committed for the quarter unless an ability says otherwise.

### Price
Every selling product chooses:
- BUDGET
- STANDARD
- PREMIUM

Players set price markers face-down and reveal simultaneously.

Appeal effect:
- Budget +2
- Standard 0
- Premium −2

Price also changes the contribution earned per sale.

---

# 19. APPEAL

Maintain each product's Appeal marker.

Appeal uses:
- Base Appeal
- Quality
- Reputation
- Trend
- Price
- Marketing
- Channel
- Awards/Bestseller
- Saturation
- applicable product/card effects

Reputation bonus:
- 0–2: +0
- 3–5: +1
- 6–8: +2
- 9–10: +3

Trend:
- Low −2
- Normal 0
- Hot +2
- Boom +4

Amazon reach: +2.

Bestseller: +1.
Award: +1.

Saturation in one territory/channel/genre:
- 1–2 competing products: 0
- 3: −1 each
- 4+: −2 each

---

# 20. SALES CLAIM

Appeal sets maximum natural quarterly Sales Claim:
- Appeal 0–1 = 1K
- 2–4 = 2K
- 5–7 = 3K
- 8–10 = 4K
- 11–13 = 5K
- 14+ = 6K

A Sales Claim is not guaranteed sales. It remains limited by market demand, stock and channel capacity.

---

# 21. RESOLVING SALES

For each territory and genre:
1. Check available demand.
2. Rank eligible products by Appeal.
3. Highest Appeal sells first up to its Sales Claim, stock and channel capacity.
4. Reduce remaining market demand.
5. Continue to the next product.

Ties:
1. lower consumer price
2. higher Quality
3. higher Reputation
4. split remaining demand as evenly as practical

There is no sales die roll.

---

# 22. CASH, REVENUE & PROFIT FROM A SALE

Example: one China-made Standard Board Game cube sells on UK Amazon at Standard price.

UK Amazon contribution = $29K.
Batch Cost of Sales = $18K.

Remove 1 Stock Cube.
Increase Cash by $29K.
Increase Revenue by $29K.
Increase current-year Profit by **$11K**.

The manufacturing Cash was already paid when the batch was ordered.

---

# 23. STOCKOUTS

If a product could sell 5K but only has 3K stock, it sells 3K.

If unmet demand remains, take a SOLD OUT marker where applicable. Remaining customers can buy competing products.

Running out can indicate strong demand, but it also hands sales to competitors.

---

# 24. PRODUCT LIFE CYCLE

Products age:
- Launch: +1 where applicable
- Growth: 0
- Mature: −1
- Decline: −2

Prototype timing:
- launch year: Launch/Growth
- next full year: Growth
- following year: Mature
- following year: Decline

A New Edition can restore an ageing product according to its development cost/rules.

---

# 25. BESTSELLER, MARKET LEADER & AWARDS

### Bestseller
Working qualification: sell 8K+ copies in a quarter across qualifying routes or meet the printed condition.
Effect: +1 product Appeal; first company Bestseller also gains +1 Reputation.

### Market Leader
Highest sales in the same genre/territory for two consecutive quarters earns Market Leader.
Effect: +1 Appeal in that territory while retained.

### Awards
Eligible Quality 4–5 products may enter visible Award opportunities. Compare Quality + Reputation + Campaign Investment + Sales Achievement. Highest wins; ties Quality then sales.

Award: +1 permanent product Appeal and +1 Company Reputation.

No award die roll.

---

# 26. COMPANY SCALE

Companies gain operational capability as they grow.

Prototype thresholds:

### Startup
Default. Two main Actions per quarter.

### Growing
$750K annual Revenue + 2 active products.
One Executive Action per year.

### Established
$1.75M annual Revenue + Reputation 4+.
One Executive Action per half-year, maximum two/year.

### International
$3.5M annual Revenue + active in at least two territories.
One Executive Action per quarter.

### Global
$5.5M annual Revenue + UK/EU/USA + Reputation 7+.
One Executive Action per quarter plus one additional Executive Action in Q4.

Executive Actions are limited operational actions and cannot normally DESIGN a brand-new product.

---

# 27. OVERHEAD & STORAGE

Base annual overhead prototype:
- Startup/low revenue: $40K
- revenue above $1M: $70K
- revenue above $3M: $120K

Portfolio Management Overhead:
- 1–2 active physical products: $0
- 3–4: +$30K
- 5–6: +$70K

Two KDP/book-only titles count as approximately one physical-product equivalent for this threshold.

Territory Management Overhead:
- UK only: $0
- UK + one foreign territory: +$30K
- UK + EU + USA: +$70K

Excess stock charge:
- first 5K unsold stock: $0
- 6K–10K: $20K
- 11K–20K: $50K
- 21K+: $100K

Warehouse upgrades may improve free capacity.

---

# 28. ANNUAL ACCOUNTS

After Q4:
1. Finish all Q4 sales and Profit movements.
2. Pay staff/service costs due.
3. Pay base overhead.
4. Pay Portfolio Management Overhead.
5. Pay Territory Management Overhead.
6. Pay storage/excess inventory charge.
7. Pay loan interest.
8. Apply other annual expenses/income.
9. Determine Pre-Tax Profit.
10. Calculate simplified corporation tax.
11. Record After-Tax Profit.
12. Check victory.
13. Check Company Scale qualification.
14. Reset Revenue and current-year Profit for the next year after recording results.
15. Advance Year and refresh annual markets.

### Prototype Tax
- positive Profit up to $250K: 20%
- amount above $250K: 25%
- loss: no corporation tax

Use the printed tax lookup aid rather than calculating percentages during final production play.

---

# 29. ADMINISTRATION

A company is not immediately eliminated when Cash reaches $0.

If unable to pay a mandatory cost, it may:
1. take available loan finance
2. liquidate stock at printed clearance value
3. sell/relinquish a product right for recovery value

If still unable to meet obligations, enter Administration:
- receive $200K rescue finance
- lose 2 Reputation
- discard one product
- take $50K restructuring expense against annual Profit
- cannot win during that same Business Year

The player remains in the game.

---

# 30. TWO-PLAYER GAME

Use the printed 2-player demand values.

Add Neutral Competitor Products in two genres each year. Neutral competitors have printed Appeal/price, consume demand, and do not take turns.

This keeps the market competitive without running an automated third company.

---

# 31. THREE- AND FOUR-PLAYER GAMES

Use the printed demand values for that player count.

Neutral competitors are normally unnecessary because real players create sufficient competition.

---

# 32. GUIDED FIRST QUARTER

Example: Dragon Forge begins with $500K.

### Business Round A
Dragon Forge secretly selects DESIGN and reveals it.
It takes **Dragon Realm**, a Fantasy Concept with Base Appeal 4, and pays the stated development cost.

### Business Round B
Dragon Forge chooses PRODUCE only if the product is production-ready; otherwise it may invest further in DESIGN/Quality, MARKET future demand, EXPAND or manage another strategic priority subject to the no-repeat rule.

Suppose another player has chosen **Party Panic**, intending a cheap Compact product.

Already the players are making different strategic commitments: Dragon Forge has a more expensive product with higher premium potential; Party Panic can reach production with less Cash tied up.

### Quarter End
Orders in production move through the pipeline. Players can see NEXT quarter's business condition before making the following quarter's decisions.

---

# 33. GUIDED SALES EXAMPLE

Q4 UK Fantasy has 12K demand remaining.

Dragon Realm:
- Appeal 13
- Sales Claim 5K
- Stock 8K

Mystic Realms:
- Appeal 11
- Sales Claim 5K
- Stock 6K

Tiny Dragons:
- Appeal 10
- Sales Claim 4K
- Stock 10K

Resolve:
- Dragon Realm sells 5K; 7K demand remains.
- Mystic Realms sells 5K; 2K remains.
- Tiny Dragons sells 2K; market is exhausted.

Tiny Dragons did not suffer a random bad roll. Its player entered a market where two stronger products captured most customers.

---

# 34. FIRST-GAME STRATEGY TIPS

- Do not spend all $500K on one enormous production run.
- China is cheap but slow.
- Europe can be worth the extra cost if timing matters.
- Budget pricing can win customers but destroys margin if overused.
- Premium pricing works best with Quality/Reputation.
- Website sales can be lucrative, but Website capacity must be built.
- KDP is low risk; physical books offer higher potential margin.
- Watch competitors' stock. Their stockout can become your opportunity.
- Do not expand internationally simply because you can; territories create overhead.
- The winner needs annual Profit, not merely the biggest warehouse or most Revenue.

---

# 35. PROTOTYPE STATUS

Rulebook v1.1 is a complete teachable baseline, but all financial values, card counts, demand tracks, Company powers, component dimensions and timing remain subject to physical playtesting.

Whenever a mechanism is reviewed, ask:

**Does this create a meaningful business decision, or merely administration/luck?**

Prototype 1.7 should use actual playtest results to revise this rulebook rather than adding complexity without evidence.
