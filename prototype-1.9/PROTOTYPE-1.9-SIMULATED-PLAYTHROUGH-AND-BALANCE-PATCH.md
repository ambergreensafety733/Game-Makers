# GAME MAKERS: The $2 Million Race
## Prototype 1.9 — Full Simulated Playthrough & Balance Patch 1

Purpose: stress-test the exact Prototype 1.8 structure before commercial artwork. This is a design simulation, not a substitute for human physical playtesting.

## 1. Test Match

Four companies, Company powers OFF:
- Dragon Forge — premium board-game strategy
- Market Rocket — China/Amazon volume
- Direct Hit — Website/direct-sales strategy
- Storyforge — books/KDP plus licensing

Starting position for each:
- Cash $500K
- Profit $0
- Revenue $0
- Reputation 1
- Website 0
- Startup Scale
- UK Amazon available

Two main Actions per quarter; same Action category cannot normally be selected twice in a quarter.

## 2. Simulated Strategic Arc

### Year 1 — Establishment
Dragon Forge spends heavily on a Quality Standard product and is cash-constrained before its China stock arrives.
Market Rocket develops a lower-cost product and commits to a larger China run.
Direct Hit invests in a product plus Website infrastructure, accepting slower first-year sales.
Storyforge develops a Game Book and uses KDP, preserving substantially more Cash.

Finding: all four openings are viable, but physical-game players need clearer development-cost values printed directly on Concept cards. Rulebook text alone is insufficient.

### Year 2 — First Separation
Market Rocket benefits from stock availability and Amazon reach but has significant Cash tied up in inventory.
Dragon Forge begins earning stronger per-unit margin after the premium product reaches market.
Direct Hit's Website starts becoming commercially useful but the EXPAND action feels expensive because it competes with PRODUCE/MARKET for scarce actions.
Storyforge generates safe book income but cannot match physical-game upside without licensing/expansion.

Finding: Website progression needs exact upgrade costs and should not require a full EXPAND action for every single level after initial launch.

### Year 3 — Portfolio Decisions
Players begin launching second/third products. Portfolio overhead now matters.
Licensing becomes attractive because it uses no stock.
European production becomes useful for reacting to visible Q4 demand.

Finding: DEALS is highly situational. If no attractive licence/retail card is visible, choosing DEALS can feel wasted. A fallback DEALS action is required.

### Year 4 — International Scale
Leading companies open EU/USA routes and combine channels. The diversified companies begin outperforming narrow specialists, but portfolio and territory overhead stop expansion being free.

Finding: International demand needs exact printed values. Approximate multipliers cannot survive into a playable ruleset.

### Year 5 — $2M Challenge
A well-run diversified/volume company can approach or exceed $2M after-tax Profit only if it has multiple mature sales engines, strong international reach and/or licences.
Pure books remain profitable but normally cannot win alone.

Finding: the victory concept works, but the final $2M climb requires larger late-game demand than the current UK-only tables provide. EU/USA tracks and retail/licensing must therefore be fully quantified before Balance Pass 2.

## 3. Major Contradictions / Missing Values Found

1. Concept development costs are not yet fully specified.
2. Quality upgrade costs are not fully specified.
3. Website upgrade costs are missing.
4. EU/USA exact demand tables are missing.
5. Direct Website contribution/margin table is incomplete.
6. Retail contract exact unit contribution is incomplete.
7. KDP contribution is stated, but physical-book direct contribution needs a printed table.
8. Custom Concept surcharge needs an exact value.
9. New Edition cost/action needs an exact value.
10. Warehouse upgrade costs/capacity need exact values.
11. Clearance/liquidation recovery values need exact values.
12. Product-right disposal value during financial distress needs exact values.
13. Award Campaign Investment needs a fixed menu rather than arbitrary spending.
14. Revenue currently tracks channel contribution rather than consumer sales; this is acceptable as a game abstraction but should be renamed 'Business Revenue' and explicitly defined.

## 4. Balance Patch 1 — Exact Prototype Values

### Development Cost by Format
Base development cost:
- Game Book: $20K
- Card Game: $30K
- Compact Game: $50K
- Standard Board Game: $80K
- Deluxe Board Game: $120K

Concept cards may modify this by ±$10K/$20K.

### Quality Cost
All products begin at Quality 2 unless the Concept says otherwise.
Upgrade during development or with DESIGN:
- Q2 -> Q3: $20K
- Q3 -> Q4: $40K
- Q4 -> Q5: $70K

Quality cannot normally be reduced to recover Cash.

### Custom Concept
DESIGN a Base Appeal 3 Custom Concept in any genre for normal format development cost + $20K.

### Website
Opening Website Level 1: EXPAND + $30K.
Upgrade costs:
- L1 -> L2: $30K
- L2 -> L3: $50K
- L3 -> L4: $80K

After Website Level 1 exists, a FINANCE/Executive operational action may purchase one upgrade instead of requiring EXPAND, subject to playtest.

Capacity remains 1K / 2K / 4K / 6K per product per quarter.

### Territory Opening
- EU: EXPAND + $60K
- USA: EXPAND + $100K

### Warehouse
Each opened territory includes 5K free annual stock capacity.
Upgrade +5K free capacity: $30K.
Second +5K: $50K.
A warehouse upgrade may be bought with EXPAND or eligible Executive Action.

### New Edition
DESIGN + $40K for Card/Compact/Book.
DESIGN + $70K for Standard/Deluxe.
Return Mature/Decline product to Growth at the start of next quarter; retain Quality.

### Financial Distress
Clearance: sell physical stock for 50% of its printed batch Cost of Sales, rounded down to nearest $5K per cube. Clearance creates Cash but no positive Profit.
Sell/relinquish a product right: receive $20K + $10K per permanent Quality level above Q2 + $10K if it holds an Award/Bestseller; discard product and associated unsold stock must still be cleared/disposed separately.

## 5. Action Economy Patch

### DESIGN fallback
If no Concept is wanted, improve Quality, create Custom Concept, or prepare New Edition.

### PRODUCE fallback
If no production is required, reposition up to 3K existing stock between opened territories by paying $10K logistics cost; arrives next quarter.

### MARKET fallback
Spend $10K on Market Research: look at the next hidden Forecast exact effect or inspect the next two Concept cards and return them in either order.

### EXPAND fallback
Improve one existing warehouse or Website level by paying the required cost.

### DEALS fallback — Business Development
If no visible Deal is taken, pay $10K to cycle one Licence OR Retail card: discard it and reveal a replacement. The player may immediately take the replacement if eligible, paying its normal costs.

### FINANCE fallback
Take/repay finance, buy an eligible Website operational upgrade, or take a $10K Cost Review: reduce this year's base overhead by $10K, maximum once per year.

This ensures every Action category has a useful decision even when the public card row is unattractive.

## 6. Direct-Sales Economics — Working Values

Use contribution received by the publisher before Cost of Sales.

- Compact: $20K / $27K / $34K
- Standard: $28K / $37K / $46K
- Deluxe: $37K / $49K / $61K
- Physical Game Book: $13K / $16K / $19K

Website contribution is intentionally higher than Amazon but capacity is limited and must be developed.

### KDP Game Book
Retain +$9K Cash/Profit per 1K as the current rounded prototype value.

## 7. Amazon Values

Retain Prototype 1.5 working UK Amazon contribution per 1K:
- Card: $11K / $15K / $19K
- Compact: $16K / $21K / $26K
- Standard: $22K / $29K / $36K
- Deluxe: $29K / $39K / $49K
- Book: $10K / $13K / $16K

## 8. Retail Simplification

Retail cards should print their own exact economics rather than use a universal retail calculation.

Recommended card format:
- required product/Quality/stock
- units purchased
- publisher contribution per 1K OR one total contract payment
- listing fee if any
- timing/territory

This is easier to play and lets different retailers feel different.

## 9. Award Simplification

Award entries use one of three fixed Campaign Investment choices:
- $0K = +0
- $20K = +1
- $40K = +2

Award score = Quality + Reputation bonus + Campaign Investment bonus + printed Sales Achievement bonus.

No arbitrary bidding and no dice.

## 10. Administration Reduction

Remove routine manual Revenue addition for tiny effects. Revenue/Business Revenue should move only when receiving sales, licence, royalty or retail income.

Use $10K or preferably $20K/$50K track increments where possible in the final board.

Do not track consumer RRP turnover separately.

## 11. First Balance Assessment

Premium physical games: viable, cash hungry, strong late margin.
China/Amazon: viable and powerful, checked by lead time/inventory.
Website: viable after exact upgrade costs; strong late margin, capacity constrained.
Books/KDP: excellent low-risk opening/support strategy, deliberately lower ceiling.
Licensing: strategically strong but dependent on Quality/Reputation and visible offers.
Europe: valuable responsive production, especially Q3/Q4.
Diversification: strongest robust late-game route but checked by portfolio overhead/action pressure.

No core route requires dice or high luck.

## 12. Strategic Interaction Check

The simulated playthrough supports the desired player thoughts:
- whether to enter an occupied genre
- whether a rival will price Budget
- whether China stock will arrive in time
- whether to bid for scarce capacity
- whether to challenge a licence
- whether to exploit a rival stockout
- whether international expansion justifies overhead

The game should continue to reveal enough information for these decisions without revealing opponents' exact next Action/price.

## 14. Artwork Gate

Do NOT commission/lock polished card art, central-board art or final box dielines yet.

Artwork may begin at concept/visual-identity level, but production layouts should wait until Prototype 2.0 numerical tables survive another balance pass.

This protects the project from paying to redesign beautiful components after economic rules change.
